# M04 · Robust strategic audit design

The baseline is T15 and T16 for full-record evaluation aliases, T22 for acquisition lower bounds, T23 for time-valid confidence and separate stopping, and T24 for history-uniform conditional-mean intervals, as corrected in its accepted review.

## M04.1 · Finite exact audit optimization

Fix a finite rooted extensive-form tree for an authorized audit. An initial hidden completion or instrument contract can be selected by the responder and can affect the initial auditor record through a declared chance law. Each initial completion has an independently fixed deployment label k in {PASS, FAIL}. This label does not depend on the selected audit design. Subsequent chance probabilities are given at concrete nodes. The auditor and responder have specified information sets, finite actions and perfect recall of their own information and actions. Legal auditor moves are common callable operations on the possible concrete nodes of each applicable information set. Rejection can be recorded when the call itself is authorized. Available operations, visible cues, administrative metadata, query and response records, stop decisions, timing or costs when visible, and any other allowed observations are represented in the tree. The responder can carry hidden memory and react to any history it sees. It cannot act on information absent from its information set, including future private auditor coins. Responder decisions describe the declared admissible nuisance. The two players' private random draws are independent, except for any explicitly modeled shared or observed signals. The public rule and tree are known to both players. Each path ends by horizon H with a forced declaration or a separately modeled abstention. Path costs are known nonnegative numbers in the tree.

The robust requirements are upper bounds α and β for the probabilities of giving a wrong label or abstaining, uniformly under all responders with initial label PASS and FAIL respectively. A separate observable or hidden path cost C is assessed either by its worst expected value or by a worst supported path budget B. An optional budget B means zero probability of C>B against every admissible responder. Other fixed terminal loss bounds may also be imposed. All labels, instruments, payoffs and admissibility restrictions must describe the real completions of interest for a real assurance claim.

1. Enumerate pure auditor and pure responder strategies (including the responder's root choice if it is free). Under independent private auditor randomization fixed before play, select a mixture of pure auditor plans. For each pure responder plan, the probability of each specified terminal error or budget violation and the expected cost are linear in these auditor weights. Enforcing the corresponding rows for all pure responder plans gives an exact linear program. Minimizing an upper bound for worst expected cost, minimizing worst failure under a specified expected-cost cap, or deciding feasibility at a fixed H and hard budget gives the corresponding optimum. If both labels are possible, the LP can impose separate risk caps. An omitted label contributes no constraint. The method works with informative initialization and non-resetting responses. A rational, feasible tree input has attained finite optima for bounded objectives and exact rational policies or infeasibility certificates. Enumerating all pure plans need not be efficient.
2. If the allowed responder strategies are precisely the behavioral strategies of the stated perfect-recall tree and their private mixtures, the same robust constraints admit a sequence-form LP polynomial in the explicit tree and rational input bit length. Let x,y be the auditor and responder realization vectors with respective constraints Ex=e, Fy=f, x,y≥0. For each terminal loss j (including class-specific failure, budget violation or cost) let A_j contain the chance-weighted terminal losses indexed by the two last sequences. Use class-specific loss zero at leaves of the other label. For cap b_j, introduce a free vector v_j and impose Fᵀv_j≥A_jᵀx and fᵀv_j≤b_j. Apply this for each cap with the common auditor x. A feasible x gives a behavioral implementation at each information set by its local realization ratios; choose arbitrary legal behavior at its zero predecessor weights. For the pure-strategy table in part 1, one may also use any explicitly listed finite response restrictions closed under their stated independent mixtures. The sequence-form representation in this part uses the actual unrestricted choices at the specified information sets, or an exact encoding of further restrictions in the tree.

## M04.2 · Exact composite obstruction for a fixed audit

Fix any such audit design before making its final label decision, possibly adaptive and with a randomized stopping rule. Let T be its finite complete terminal record available to the test, including informative initialization, actions, allowed responses and retained auditor information. Terminal declarations are excluded from T. Let C_+ and C_− be the convex hulls of the PASS and FAIL record laws for that design. Assume both are nonempty. They are compact polytopes in the finite tree model. The same analytic condition holds for any two nonempty compact convex families of laws on a finite record set.

A randomized terminal test can satisfy the uniform class failures α and β for forced binary decisions if and only if for every λ∈[0,1], P∈C_+, Q∈C_−,
Σ_{t∈T} min(λP(t),(1−λ)Q(t)) ≤ λα+(1−λ)β.
At any violation, the two class mixtures and λ witness infeasibility. The worst excess equals the maximum of the left side minus the right side. For finite rational input this maximum is a linear program in class subprobability weights and common overlap masses. The criterion also holds for feasibility when abstention is allowed but charged as failure in each class. In particular intersecting hulls force maximum class failure at least 1/2, even when no opposite pure laws agree. Equal full-record laws remain the pairwise special case. Disjoint hulls alone do not imply feasibility for prescribed α,β below 1/2.

## M04.3 · Interval responders and stopping-valid confidence

Take the accepted T24 scoring class, with a finite candidate set, globally repeatable legal binary trusted probes Q of fixed positive costs, and closed per-completion/probe intervals I_{cq}. After each predictable probe selection and on every reached legal audit history, the actual score has conditional mean within its interval. Sufficiency allows history-dependent hidden memory and needs only these bounds. For the converse require that for an opposite pair with overlapping intervals on all probes, a common choice of Bernoulli means from the intersections is realized by jointly admissible stationary score responders in both worlds, with common full initialization and joint response record laws along those choices. This is automatic in the score-only class with common initialization and independent auditor coins when all stationary selections of means are admitted. Common auxiliary marginals without matching joint records do not suffice. An independently enrolled honest bit V with conditional mean in [a,b], plus a checked conditional mismatch rate Pr(Z≠V | h,q)≤η at all relevant post-query histories, supplies the interval [a−η,b+η] clipped to [0,1].

1. For δ∈(0,1/2), a finite-cost audit with uniform failure (including abstention) at most δ exists in this class exactly when every opposite-label pair has disjoint score intervals somewhere. At the nth score from q use radius b(n)=sqrt(log(2|Q| n(n+1)/δ)/(2n)) around its sample mean when there is an opposite pair. Retain each candidate only while its corresponding interval is at distance at most b(n) from every sampled mean for that probe and count. For each separating q, choose n_q with 2b(n_q) below the smallest strictly positive opposite-class interval gap on q. Use one separating probe for a remaining opposite pair until reaching its quota or a terminal set. The rule has worst cost at most Σ_q c(q)n_q over separating probes, with zero if the label is initially homogeneous. The finite score-only unrestricted history-wise interval class up to a cap can be represented by choices of the interval endpoints followed by the Bernoulli chance draw. The exact finite design optimum is then covered by M04.1, subject to the stated representation assumptions.
2. For a fixed audit design and a finite record alphabet at each depth, let P range over its declared (possibly composite) complete record laws. Let M be any fixed consistent reference law of the same padded-record space. Retain a law P at a prefix h where P(h)>0 and M(h)/P(h)<1/δ, or use intersection with all previous such sets. For every admitted actual law P the chance that the truth is ever removed is at most δ. Nonempty class-homogeneous issuance has unconditional wrong-issuance probability at most δ at any adapted stop. In M04.1 a reference mixture of extreme laws is available and each finite-history feasibility test with all past retained inequalities is linear in transcript-law weights, with strict inequalities checked by finite slack. A set of possible laws with no member may be flagged as incompatible with the recorded tests. This procedure provides no general deadline, issuance probability or guarantee of detecting all omitted laws.

## M04.4 · Expected-cost horizon approximation

Suppose additionally that an infinite audit model has finite branching and perfect recall at each stage, fixed latent label, finite information alphabets at each finite depth, admissible arbitrary adapted behavioral policies, and finite-horizon classes obtained by consistent truncation. Every finite-horizon allowed responder extends to the infinite game, and all infinite responders restrict to the finite classes. Forcing a label at the cap changes only the terminal decision there and gives no prior cue to the model. Each completed probe costs at least c_0>0, all costs are accumulated nonnegatively, stopping itself costs zero, and each continuation takes another probe. An infinite audit must stop almost surely against each admissible responder.

Fix a finite worst expected cost cap B and class failures α,β. If an infinite audit meets these requirements, forced stopping at H after at most H probes gives a finite audit with worst expected cost at most B and class errors at most α+B/(c_0(H+1)), β+B/(c_0(H+1)). Conversely, if for every ε>0 some finite-cap audit has worst expected cost at most B and errors at most α+ε and β+ε, there exists an almost surely stopping infinite audit with the exact B,α,β guarantees. This concerns the expected-cost feasible frontier. It need not give a finite deterministic stopping time at the exact error pair. Finite rational programs approximate the error bounds at a specified positive slack when an infinite feasible protocol exists.
