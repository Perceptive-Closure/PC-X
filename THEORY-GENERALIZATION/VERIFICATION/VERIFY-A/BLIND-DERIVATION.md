## M06 · T29.J

### Supported minimax and finite decision

At a permitted public history with nonempty belief \(B\), the meter \(p\) and all past charges are fixed by the record. If command \(a\) is executable at every member, every listed outcome \(y\) with a live predecessor is attainable. Its next belief is exactly the union of successors on that record. For the forward direction every compatible concrete run contributes its successor. Conversely, any edge used in that union can be appended to an actual prefix reaching its predecessor by the exact conditional row premise. In particular, the branchwise residual work \(b-w_y\) is the same for all states in that branch. Tags stay fixed.

Handle an empty initial belief as inconsistent and an unsafe initial belief as unsafe. In continuation states a reachable unsafe state or unsafe edge defeats the policy, even if the target is already unanimous. A row with an unsafe successor can either be rejected explicitly or lead to an unsafe losing child. At a nonempty safe belief, a lawful verdict costs zero. Without a valid stop, horizon zero loses. At positive horizon, any successful policy must choose a safe, common command with each supported work charge affordable. Every outcome supplies a separate continuation with depth at most \(h-1\). The lower bound is the maximum over those outcomes of \(c_y\) plus the optimal continuation value. For the converse, choose a minimizing command and independently attach minimizing continuations to the public outcomes. All costs are nonnegative, so a valid stop dominates further moves. An empty minimum has value \(+\infty\). This proves the recurrence and attainment with a finite callable catalog. Cost is maximized after adding the two terms on **the same outcome**.

A randomized policy under a supported objective has to cover each supported action at each reached history and every supported physical branch afterward. Choosing one admissible supported action and its covered continuations at each history gives the same bound with a deterministic tree. A finite refutation of budget \(c\) starts at \((B,p,h,b,c)\) and covers every callable command. Record a legality, safety or work rejection, or select one genuinely supported branch whose residual acquisition \(c-c_y\) is negative or whose child is refuted. A valid stop is excluded at each refutation node. Horizon reduction gives soundness and termination. A finite alphabet with checkable charges suffices for effective replay. At fixed \(p,b,h\) a tree can be restricted to any nonempty subbelief, retaining legal commands and all still-supported continuations, so its worst cost cannot increase.

With integer budgets, use configurations \((B,p,b,c)\). Seed the winning set with lawful stops, then add any safe configuration with a common lawful affordable action whose every child is already in the set. There are at most \(L=|P|(2^{|S|}-1)(B_0+1)(C_0+1)\) configurations. Choose the action used to first insert each configuration. Every move strictly lowers the insertion rank, giving depth at most \(L\) even with zero-cost cycles. Outside the final set, each affordable lawful common command leaves some supported child outside. Such choices yield a losing finite history or an infinite history. In the latter case the finitely branching concrete tree of prefixes consistent with that history has a run by König's lemma, since every finite prefix is realized. A zero-cost loop alone cannot justify a proper success.

### Correspondence and witnesses

A causal, observation-consistent interpretation takes the same physical command throughout a compatible concrete cell and maps each actual public record to the designated abstract record. The forward invariant requires a related abstract initial possibility and a covering successor on each concrete supported transition. The costs and goal labels used by the certificate hold on its physical executions. These facts make a robust abstract policy sound even when it handles extra abstract successors, provided its chosen actions have the required physical lifts. Equality of optima needs the converse policy simulation as well. In particular, the physical controller's actionable distinctions have to be available in the exact model up to an observable re-encoding.

For an observation counterexample, take two positive tags with a zero-cost read of the tag and two useful commands, each executable only at its own tag. With two moves the physical policy reads and then serves at cost zero. An abstraction can preserve the two state transitions in both directions and keep the same commands and costs while merging the two exact readout values to one label. On that quotient the belief stays mixed across the two positive tags and no common service command becomes available. Both-way edge coverage under this coarsening cannot establish the exact optimum.

For an interleaving witness, take tags with \(f=0,1\) and a single common command that records either a lawful negative verdict cell at charges \((1,0)\) or a useful positive terminal at charges \((2,1)\). Add an ordinary read at cost 3 and a separate job authorized only on tag 1. With one move and work budget 1 the joint optimum is 2. With work budget 0 it is infinite. With a pure read followed by the job the worst acquisition is 3. For the three-tag witness, use three commands, each enabled on the other two tags and moving to a useful terminal. Every pair wins in one move and the triple loses despite unanimous \(f=1\). An initial meter that reveals a menu would change that belief and the example. An unrecorded tariff cannot be subtracted from a public budget as if it were known. An unobserved carry that changes rows belongs in the hidden state and a disclosed carry belongs in the public state.

**Qualification on the literal seal.** Individually finite rows need a finite exhaustive set of callable command names for the stated attainment and finite enumeration certificates. If arbitrary names are admitted, let a single pending positive state have commands \(a_j\), \(j\ge1\), each taking it safely to a useful terminal in one move at acquisition \(1+1/j\) and work zero. The lower value is an infimum of 1 with no attaining command. This gap disappears for the intended finite catalog. A pure externally deployable read cell and joint readiness remain separate objectives, as do mutable targets and positive-risk control.

The knowledge-state reduction for sure objectives is established. The present use attaches public tariffs, verdicts and actual useful completion to concrete callable rows. [Chatterjee, Doyen, Henzinger and Raskin, 2007, `3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.2619)

## M07 · T07.K

The atomic context fast job merges the other pair exactly if \(x=k\). The slow counterparts preserve both partitions. Job events advance the phase regardless of that guarded extensional effect, and their ordinary source authority and tariff are fixed conditional facts. Take four normalized representatives with nonempty compatible fibers, two per block. Use a representative from the gateway block at entry and one from the context block after the first job. Their admission history can advance with the phase while the independently stored equivalence blocks govern the R effect. The guard reads \(g\) or computes an equality with \(k\) inside the interpreter. The certificate checks both conditional branches without giving the auditor the installed registers or a precomputed live menu. Frozen runs retain only the lawful full command occurrences and always keep the slow actions. A phase and the current context are execution data. The latent installation itself needs \(n+1\) bits. Each point call conveys an \(n\)-bit address and receives one bit. Identity outputs \(n+1\) bits.

For \(g=0\), the single public rule “fast gateway, then slow context” executes both real jobs at work cost 3 for every admitted \(x\) and every \(k\). For \(g=1\), mandatory slow gateway costs 3, and even a fast context costs at least another 1. Moving the exceptional key changes actual frozen availability and the known-mode minimum tariff at either changed context, although it leaves the decision target fixed. ### Noiseless hard caps and expected bounds

The gateway read identifies the target in one call. Without a call the observations are identical for both target values, so uniform success is at most \(1/2\). This is a hard worst-path result. If one instead optimizes expected calls at fixed wrong-or-abstain risk \(\delta\), the exact noiseless target cost is \(1-2\delta\). Reading with probability \(1-2\delta\) and otherwise guessing fairly attains it. Under an independent uniform input gateway bit, the decision ever to query it depends only on coins and key answers up to that first gateway query. Without it the average success probability is at most \(1/2\). Thus attaining success at least \(1-\delta\) requires spending a gateway call with probability at least \(1-2\delta\).

For identity, the upper bound at integer cap \(1\le q\le N\) reads the gateway, selects a uniformly random permutation of keys, tests its first \(q-1\) keys, then guesses the next if all tested keys miss. Each key lies among the first \(q\) with probability \(q/N\), for every input. At cap zero, a uniformly random pair attains \(1/(2N)\). At \(q=N\) the last key is inferred, giving deterministic exact identification by using a fixed ordering.

For a matching upper bound on success, fix the random coins and count correct inputs among the \(2N\) uniformly weighted pairs. It suffices to treat \(1\le q<N\). Follow the transcript with all point misses through the first gateway query, or until it stops without that query. Repeated point calls along this transcript need never be issued in an exact protocol. If the first gateway query follows \(s\) point misses, at most \(2s\) pairs can be correct off this transcript before the gateway. Once it is read, each fixed-gateway branch has at most \(q-s-1\) further point calls. A point-only decision tree can be correct on at most its cap plus one keys, since a first positive answer singles out one key and the all-negative transcript contributes at most one more. Hence at most \(2s+2(q-s)=2q\) pairs are correct. If this all-miss transcript stops after \(s<q\) points with no gateway read, it contributes at most one correct pair and the earlier isolated keys contribute at most \(2s\). If it uses all \(q\) calls, the key whose first positive answer comes on the last call can contribute at most one pair, as can the all-negative transcript. The earlier keys then contribute at most \(2(q-1)\). These counts also apply to abstention. Averaging over coins leaves the uniform-pair average at most \(q/N\), so the minimum input success probability has the same upper bound. At cap zero the count is at most one pair. The exact zero-error worst cost \(N\) follows.

If an identity policy has total acquisition \(T\) with \(\sup_{g,k}\mathbb E T=t<\infty\), truncate at \(\ell\) paid calls, abstaining when more are needed. For every input the probability of preserving a correct original answer is at least \(1-\delta-\Pr(T>\ell)\ge1-\delta-t/\ell\). The hard-cap upper bound gives \(t\ge \ell(1-\delta-\ell/N)\) for every integer \(\ell\ge1\). Taking \(\ell=N/2\) gives \(t\ge(N/2)(1/2-\delta)\) for fixed \(\delta<1/2\).

### Shared noisy answer regime

For a fixed queried bit, sum \(+1\) for an answer of 1 and \(-1\) for 0. Stop at upper boundary \(U>0\) or lower boundary \(-L<0\), with integer \(U,L\), and return the boundary's bit. At a transcript with sum \(s\) the likelihood ratio of true-bit 1 to true-bit 0 is \(r^s\). On the erroneous lower boundary, summing the stopped transcript masses under bit 1 gives at most \(r^{-L}\). At the erroneous upper boundary the bound under bit 0 is \(r^{-U}\).

Let \(T\) be the boundary hitting time. Under bit 1, for finite \(m\) the mean stopped sum at \(T\wedge m\) equals \(\gamma \mathbb E(T\wedge m)\) by summing conditional drifts before stopping. The sum never exceeds \(U\), giving \(\mathbb E T\le U/\gamma\) by monotone convergence. Under bit 0 use the negated sum to get \(\mathbb E T\le L/\gamma\). These estimates prove almost-sure termination for each test. They apply conditional on all records at a test's start. This is the elementary unit-step symmetric-channel instance of a sequential likelihood-ratio test. [Wald, 1945](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731118)

Take \(U=L=a\) for the gateway. Its risk is at most \(\delta/3\) and its worst expectation at most \(a/\gamma\). For identity, run the gateway test and a separate \((L,U)=(a,b)\) test at **each** key. Return the gateway bit and the unique key declared positive, with abstention when the context declarations are inconsistent. The error or abstention event is contained in the union of gateway error, rejection of the true key, and acceptance of any false key. The bound is \(2r^{-a}+(N-1)r^{-b}\le\delta\). The expected total is at most \(\{a+(N-1)a+b\}/\gamma\). The promised conditional flip rule and independent simulated flips give exactly the same adaptive transcript in an exact-oracle simulation, at the same call count. Consequently the expected lower bound transfers to noise. Fixed \(\delta,\eta\) give \(\Theta(N)\) identity acquisition and a constant target upper bound. If proper termination is required on every supported answer path, cap the audit and abstain after the cap. The error bound above has slack at least \(\delta/(3N)\) for identity and \(2\delta/3\) for the gateway, so a sufficiently large finite cap preserves the risk by Markov's inequality and can only reduce expected calls. Neither argument transfers the tight noiseless hard cap to the noisy oracle.

A genuinely callable exact deterministic identifying menu with known total cost gives that same upper bound by sequential readout of it and inversion of its answer vector. A noisy menu needs the stipulated identification fidelity or enough charged repetitions, since a raw bit flipped with probability \(\eta\) cannot attain \(\delta<\eta\) in one call. Any free installed-key disclosure, work trial, simultaneous read or arbitrary group query changes the information interface.

## M08 · T30.CG

### Exact compressed update

For a fixed path, choosing fast at bit 0 and slow at bit 1 attains \(\kappa(e)\) per stage, and every permitted completion of the required job costs at least that much. Reads do not change the relevant availability or tariff, so its intrinsic support-robust work is the sum of these stage minima. The initialization is common, selects whole first-edge cells, or is incorporated into a refined graph that represents exactly the compatible paths.

Set \(C=D+1\) and define capped addition by \(u\oplus v=\min(C,u+v)\). It is associative and nondecreasing, and nonnegative tariffs mean crossing \(D\) can be remembered by \(C\). Fix a public history at current edge layer \(i\). For each feasible current edge \(e\), consider all compatible path prefixes reaching \(e\). Any such prefix can be extended by **any** accepting suffix from the head of \(e\). This holds initially by the compatible graph and survives each successive record. A read filters only by the supported public observation and new meter at the current edge. A job filters by the same local test, consumes that edge, and permits every next edge sharing its head vertex. Neither action looks into a later suffix, given \(e,p\). The same argument shows each compatible prefix has a physical realization of the fixed history. Parallel edges remain distinct.

The common command set is the intersection over present edges. Form groups by the **complete** public record, using the next meter as a branch coordinate if it is needed to disambiguate updates. For a read, keep precisely the edges supporting the record. For a job, shift each retained edge's two extrema by \(\oplus\kappa(e)\). For each next edge take the minimum and maximum among shifted predecessors with the required vertex. Monotonicity of \(\oplus\) makes these the extrema of the actual union. The terminal aggregation applies the same last-edge shift and forgets the last vertex when there is no edge-indexed move at the terminal. Initially all feasible first edges have prefix extrema \((0,0)\).

Compute minimum and maximum capped suffix tariffs at every vertex by a backward DAG pass, using zero at the accepting terminals. For each present edge join a prefix extremum, its tariff and the corresponding suffix extremum. Then minimize the minimum and maximize the maximum across the present edges. Each extreme is attained by concatenating a compatible prefix and real suffix at that edge. If the global maximum is at most \(D\), the cell is positive. If the global minimum is \(C\), the cell is negative. Otherwise representatives show both targets. No intermediate score has been inferred. Witnesses for a particular history must use its own compatible prefixes, reconstructed by replay and backpointers. A shared memo entry is a value/transition equivalence and does not make a representative from one history valid for every reaching history.

### Minimax, verification and complexity

With \(h\) moves and \(d\) work left, a nonempty all-negative cell allows immediate NO. YES requires the terminal and an all-positive cell. On actual within-budget terminal executions all compatible paths are positive, since the work actually paid is at least their individual intrinsic terms. An empty graph at initialization gives inconsistency. At all other states, apply the T29.J minimax with prices set by the public meter, appropriate command and cost checks, and a decrement of \(h\). Given the frontier update above, every branch agrees with the exact physical cell and each selected command can be called there by name. Both lower and upper bounds transfer. Infinite or over-cap values admit finite losing certificates covering all commands with unavailable/unaffordable rows or supported losing children. A mixed cell supplies opposed paths, which can witness invalid stopping. A pure positive cell before the end can also lose for operational reasons.

There are \(D+2\) capped values, and \(K=1+(D+2)(D+3)/2\) choices of an ordered pair of extrema or absence per edge. Thus the \(P\) layer-meter combinations give a bound \((H+1)(D+1)P K^{\max(1,W)}\), including the aggregate terminal. The factor for the two budgets is for moves and **work**. Acquisition is the computed value. Processing each compressed state scans explicit commands and row support, does capped additions and vertex joins, and compares rational accumulated prices exactly. A denominator dividing the product of all input denominators suffices, and total charge is at most \(H\) times the largest price on any successful branch. The bit sizes are therefore polynomial in the table encoding and \(\log(H+1)\). Materializing an expanded policy, refutation or witness for a specific history also costs its output size. Fixed width gives pseudo-polynomial dependence on numeric \(D,H\). The ambient comparisons are the enumerated path representation and the unrestricted subsets of edge/score pairs. For example, with two parallel bit alternatives per layer, linear public-meter count and \(D,H=O(n)\), the representation may have \(2^n\) accepting paths while the stated frontier bound is polynomial. The bound gives no universal speed ordering against an independently designed symbolic solver. Source-row and event-law anchors are premises external to this count.

A strict scope contrast uses two accepting paths with bit words \(01\) and \(10\), \(a_i=w_i=1\), \(D=3\), no reads, and deterministic opaque job receipts. Both scores are 3, so an external pure certificate needs no acquisition. Both the first and second common job must be slow, making joint success infeasible. A lawful first-layer bit read at price 1 and \(H=3\) makes the joint value exactly 1 by determining the whole path before service. In this example even a first-job receipt that distinguishes the paths arrives too late to guarantee useful service, since the \(01\) path spent 2 at its first stage and needs 2 at its second.

**Scope of initialization and primitive rows.** The proof needs all feasible prefixes and suffixes at a frontier edge to concatenate within the allowed graph. A common initialization, or one which restricts only whole first-edge cells, meets this requirement. If a meter at initialization were allowed to disclose a strict suffix event given the current edge, its compatible graph would have to be refined. With one common first edge of bit 0 and two second edges of bits 0 and 1, \(a_i=w_i=1\) and \(D=2\), a public initial report of the second bit would make the cell pure, while unchanged suffix extrema would give a mixed result. This tests a broader reading of the seal's local-future condition. Also the fast action must be an ordinarily lawful enabled complete job at every compatible checkpoint of bit 0. A resource effect test alone would leave open an independent guard failure and could invalidate the claimed intrinsic score.

**Scope of zero probability.** Fix a compatible installation. For a finite audit tree, every supported bad branch from it has strictly positive probability under any row law that is positive conditionally at every reachable history. Conversely, an action tree without a bad supported branch has zero failure probability. This proves the equivalence pointwise over compatible installations or after placing positive initial mass on each. Conditional row positivity without the initial support condition is weaker. For two one-job paths of bits 0 and 1 with \(a_1=w_1=D=1\), an immediate NO fails at bit 0. Probability of a harmful event at a nonzero scale needs probability information beyond support.

For each opposed pair let its disagreement set consist of the menu queries that give different answers on it. A static subset resolves the target exactly when it hits each such set. A passing candidate is optimal since its accumulated-constraint optimum was a lower bound for the full problem. This separates the outer optimization from the stated paired-oracle run time. ## M08 · Positive-risk boundary

Make two installed finite laws for a callable job that always completes usefully and emits the same receipt. In each law its unobserved actual event \(V\) has support \(\{0,1\}\). Use probabilities \(\Pr(V=1)=1/4\) and \(3/4\), respectively, with target \(\mathbf 1\{\Pr(V=1)\le1/2\}\). The source identifies the complete support and actual event semantics but does not establish the installed weights or give a readout of \(V\). Give all other permitted responses the same laws and transitions in the public protocol, with no event-dependent visible carry. Repeated completed jobs can use independent hidden draws. This makes the **full** observable law equal for every permitted adaptive policy, including service requests. Both installations offer real useful work.

With identical observable laws and common private randomization, the probabilities of returning the two opposed verdicts sum to at most 1 under that common output law. At least one installation has wrong-or-abstain risk at least \(1/2\). Equal individual benchmark marginals alone do not establish this for joint protocols. For example, two commands can each give a fair bit on either installation while a permitted sequence gives \((U,U)\) on one and \((U,1-U)\) on the other, for a persistent fair bit \(U\). The two-command records then separate them. Deployment risk requires the scored event and appropriate law, and the general positive-risk, adaptive-deployment and strategic problems remain open in this packet.
