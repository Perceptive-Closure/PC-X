## M04.1 Finite optimization and information timing

For each admissible pure auditor plan \(a\) and pure responder plan \(r\), sum chance-weighted terminal losses over the paths following both plans. Call the result \(M^j_{ar}\) for loss \(j\). Fix a probability vector \(p\) over auditor plans. The loss against responder \(r\) is \(\sum_a p_a M^j_{ar}\). With independent private randomization, the loss against a responder mixture is a weighted average of such rows. Thus each robust cap is equivalent to its linear constraints over all relevant pure \(r\), using rows from the declared response class. Fixed finite admissible restrictions that are closed under the stated mixtures can instead use the listed generators and their actual induced paths. Failure indicators can include abstentions, and the hard path budget is the cap zero for \(1_{\{C>B\}}\). An epigraph variable gives worst expected cost or worst class failure as an objective. Finite costs and a bounded horizon give attained minima, and with rational coefficients these are rational LPs with replayable policies, optimum checks or infeasibility certificates.

A plan specifies one move at each information set, including responses to earlier observations. The responder can condition on earlier observable moves but cannot condition on the private auditor plan unless the tree gives it that information. An exact timing control is the loss \(1_{\{A=G\}}\) when the auditor selects \(A\in\{0,1\}\) and the responder selects \(G\in\{0,1\}\). If the responder's two nodes have the same information set, the best auditor mixture gives loss \(1/2\) by balancing the two guesses. If it observes \(A\), its plan can match both branches and the value is \(1\).

For sequence form, perfect recall gives each information set a common predecessor sequence of that player's own recorded moves. Use nonnegative variables for the extending sequences and the equation that they sum to the predecessor realization weight at each information set, with the empty sequence normalized to one. The constraints are exactly the behavioral realization constraints. Taking each continuation probability as its realization weight divided by the predecessor's weight implements these constraints by induction where the denominator is positive. At zero-weight predecessors an arbitrary legal choice is harmless. A mixed private plan gives the same realization weight for a sequence as the probability of following it, and the same local ratios then preserve every terminal law under every opponent. Independent behavioral randomization also produces a mixture by sampling plans before play.

At a leaf the probability is the product of the chance-path weight and the two last-sequence realization weights. Group the leaves by those sequences to obtain \(x^\top A_j y\). For a fixed \(x\) the worst responder loss is the maximization over the nonempty bounded polytope \(Fy=f,\ y\ge0\). LP duality gives
\[
\max_{y\ge0,\ Fy=f} x^\top A_j y
=\min_{v:\ F^\top v\ge A_j^\top x} f^\top v.
\]
Thus each cap has the stated dual row block on the same \(x\). The number of information sets, sequences and leaf contributions is bounded by the explicit tree size, and the chance products, grouped coefficients and rational costs have polynomial bit length. The formulation assumes the real responder strategy set is the one encoded by the tree or an exactly realized restriction. [Koller, Megiddo and von Stengel's sequence-form construction](https://ai.stanford.edu/~koller/Papers/Koller%2Bal%3AGEB96.pdf)

Using zero loss outside each class is valid when a pure responder, including its initial choice, fixes the latent label, and chance for initialization gives cues within that label. Even if private mixtures cross labels, conditioning on that label keeps a mixture of the fixed-class pure rows. If the statement is read as allowing nature to randomize the label inside a pure plan, class-conditional guarantees need a modified row. For instance make PASS have chance \(1/4\) and FAIL \(3/4\), give no information and require PASS error at most \(1/4\) and FAIL error zero. Always declaring FAIL satisfies the unnormalized masked row while violating the conditional PASS guarantee. The conditional caps are infeasible. With label-randomizing chance, the correct linear constraint uses loss \(1_{\{k\}}(\mathit{failure}-\alpha_k)\) with cap zero for each label \(k\). This also handles zero class mass.

## M04.2 Composite obstruction

Write \(u(t)\in[0,1]\) for the terminal probability of declaring FAIL. The class errors are \(Pu\) for PASS and \(Q(1-u)\) for FAIL. For a fixed test the worst constraint excess is the maximum over the two class suprema of their errors after subtracting the respective caps. It is equivalently the maximum of
\[
\sum_t \{\lambda P(t)u(t)+(1-\lambda)Q(t)(1-u(t))\}-\lambda\alpha-(1-\lambda)\beta
\]
over \(\lambda,P,Q\), since the endpoints \(\lambda=0,1\) are permitted. The parametrization by subprobabilities \((\lambda P,(1-\lambda)Q)\) gives a compact convex domain when the class sets are compact convex. Convexity follows by mixing subprobabilities and normalizing each class's resulting mass when positive. The test cube and this domain satisfy bilinear minimax. Swap minimization over \(u\) with maximization over these subprobabilities. Minimizing each coordinate \(u(t)\) independently gives the smaller of the two weighted masses. The minimum worst excess is therefore exactly the stated expression, and feasibility is its nonpositivity. [Sion's minimax theorem](https://msp.org/pjm/1958/8-1/pjm-v8-n1-p14-s.pdf)

For finite law lists, give each PASS and FAIL generator nonnegative weight, summing to one across the two classes. Their class totals are \(\lambda\) and \(1-\lambda\). For each record \(t\), constrain a nonnegative common overlap variable above by each class's weighted mass at \(t\). Maximize the sum of the overlap variables minus \(\alpha\) times PASS weight minus \(\beta\) times FAIL weight. The maximizing overlap variable is exactly the smaller mass. Complete law generators come from the declared fixed design against each pure response plan. Finite rational law lists yield an exact LP. Replacing any abstention by a binary declaration weakly improves both class failures because abstention incurs failure for either class.

If the hulls intersect, equal opposite-class mixture laws make the two forced-binary error probabilities sum to one, giving worst failure at least \(1/2\). For an example, PASS pure laws are \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\), and FAIL has the sole law \((1/2,1/2)\). The hulls intersect without an equal pure opposite pair. An equal opposite pair is the one-point special case. For disjoint hulls use the two singleton laws \((3/4,1/4)\) and \((1/4,3/4)\). At \(\lambda=1/2\) their weighted overlap is \(1/4\). Class caps both \(1/8\) fail, though the hulls are disjoint. All arguments use the task's complete record after the design, including retained private information and informative initialization.

## M04.3 Strategic scores and stopping-valid retention

1. With the true completion fixed, let \(\mu_t\) be the conditional score mean after the predictable choice of probe \(q_t\). For a probe \(q\), write \(N_q(t)=\sum_{s\le t}1_{\{q_s=q\}}\). Conditional bounded-difference exponential moments make
   \[
   \exp\!\left(\lambda\sum_{s\le t}1_{\{q_s=q\}}(Z_s-\mu_s)-\lambda^2 N_q(t)/8\right)
   \]
   a nonnegative supermartingale. At the first time \(N_q(t)=n\), the empirical score lying \(b\) above the true interval's upper edge entails centered sum at least \(nb\). The nonnegative supermartingale maximal bound with \(\lambda=4b\) controls that crossing by \(e^{-2nb^2}\), even when the random \(n\)-th visit never occurs on some paths. The lower edge follows with the opposite sign. At radius \(b(n)\), sum the two-tail bounds over probes and visit counts, using \(\sum_{n\ge1}1/(n(n+1))=1\). This removes the true completion with probability at most \(\delta\) over the whole audit. [Hoeffding on bounded exponential moments](https://www.cs.rpi.edu/academics/courses/spring06/random/hoefding.pdf) · [Howard et al. on nonnegative supermartingale bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03204)

   At a probe's quota, any two opposite-class intervals with gap larger than \(2b(n_q)\) cannot both be within \(b(n_q)\) of the same realized mean. Thus a still-retained opposite pair always has an available separating probe whose quota has yet to be used. Run it up to its quota or until the stopping condition. No probe exceeds its quota, label-homogeneous roots stop for zero, and by the cumulative quota every path stops with a homogeneous or empty set. On the common valid event the set is nonempty and contains the true label. The stated worst cost and failure bounds follow. For the converse use the jointly admitted common stationary selections for an opposite pair. They induce identical full initialization and conditional record laws for every chosen probe at matching histories, hence equal full transcript laws for any audit. Its two class failures sum to at least one, including abstention. Common fair marginals alone allow an opposite pair with a score \(Z\) and cue \(U\) each fair, but \(U=Z\) in one class and \(U=1-Z\) in the other. One trial identifies from their joint law despite overlapping score intervals.

   For a checked honest bit \(V\), \(|\mathbb E[Z\mid h,q]-\mathbb E[V\mid h,q]|\le \Pr(Z\ne V\mid h,q)\), giving the clipped interval after adding the rate bound to both sides. For the finite rectangular score-only model, privately mix the endpoints at each permitted responder history to realize any interval mean, then draw the Bernoulli score with that endpoint probability. Conversely any such mixture stays in its interval conditional on the history. This reproduces all conditional score kernels up to the cap when responder information and the contract permit those per-history selections. Auxiliary cues, coupled means and restrictions on responder knowledge require their own exact tree encoding.
2. For a law \(P\), at a prefix with \(P(h)>0\), set \(L(h)=M(h)/P(h)\). The conditional expectation over the next symbol is
   \[
   \mathbb E_P[L(ha)\mid h]=\sum_{a:P(ha)>0}M(ha)/P(h)\le L(h).
   \]
   Starting at the empty record \(L=1\). Padding and the declared full initialization let the same argument run through stopped transcripts. The maximal inequality implies \(P\{\sup_h L(h)\ge1/\delta\}\le\delta\). The actual law's own zero-probability prefixes occur with probability zero, so its removal at any time has probability at most \(\delta\), whether a single current set or the running intersection is used. A wrong nonempty homogeneous issuance at any adapted stop requires this removal. [Ville's maximal inequality](https://www.numdam.org/item/THESE_1939__218__1_0.pdf)

   In the finite tree keep laws indexed by their compatible class to retain any full-law alias across labels. A fixed mixture over finite generators is an available reference. For each observed prefix \(h\), retention is \(P(h)>\delta M(h)\). It is linear in trial class-law weights with a strict comparison. An exact finite absence check maximizes a common slack added to every constraint and tests whether the optimum slack is strictly positive. This may establish incompatibility with the tested reference family, with no general finite issuance or omitted-world detection guarantee.

## M04.4 Expected cost, truncation and compactness

For an infinite audit with probe count \(N\) and total cost \(C\), \(C\ge c_0 N\). Keep its moves through \(H\) probes and force a declaration only if it would continue. This changes no earlier observed cues by assumption. Against a finite responder use its guaranteed extension and compare plays through the cap. Expected expenditure cannot increase. Error can increase only when \(N\ge H+1\), which has probability at most \(B/[c_0(H+1)]\) by Markov. Apply this in both classes.

Conversely, take a sequence of finite audits for slack tending to zero. Regard each as an infinite adapted behavioral audit that stops at its finite cap, with arbitrary legal behavior elsewhere. The cap's terminal action must be available as the corresponding stop rule under the declared consistent truncation. Finite branching and alphabets give countably many auditor information sets, each with a finite-dimensional compact behavior simplex. Extract a subsequence converging on each information set by diagonal compactness. The limiting coordinates define one admissible behavioral audit. For any fixed infinite responder and any finite prefix horizon, probabilities of terminal failures by that horizon and expected costs accumulated by then are continuous in the finitely many relevant audit coordinates. The responder's restrictions are admissible in the finite games. Each prefix bound thus passes to the limit. The same subsequence works against all responders, since it was chosen on the auditor's coordinates before fixing an opponent. Monotone convergence in the prefix horizon gives expected total cost at most \(B\) and probability of an eventual erroneous stopped report at most its exact class cap. An infinite nonstopping path would have infinitely many probes costing at least \(c_0\) each, so the finite expected bound makes nonstopping null against each responder.

A rational control has PASS emit a revealing \(p\) with probability \(1/2\) on every unit-price read and a common question mark otherwise. FAIL emits \(f\) instead of \(p\). The first revealing mark gives an exact label at mean cost two and with almost sure termination. For every finite cap the all-question-mark history has positive mass in both classes and forces positive error in at least one. This shows why an exact expected-cost frontier can require unbounded support of the stopping time. For a positive error slack, finite truncation gives an explicit sufficient cap at the same expected budget. If the finite trees and caps have rational encodings, the finite rational LPs above certify feasible approximations at that slack. The truncation and compactness claims hold for real coefficients, while an effective rational LP claim requires an effective finite data encoding.
