# M03 primary-source comparisons

## Partial identification and transport

Balke and Pearl (1997), §3, derive observable probability equalities for latent response types under randomized assignment and their instrumental-variable model, then optimize a linear causal contrast over the latent probability polytope under nonnegativity and normalization. Endpoints are attained by feasible response-type distributions. M03.1 uses the same partial-identification optimization principle with different primitives: scaled conditional reference/deployment bridge variables, uncertain event channels, and extra joint mass constraints. The minimum of a finite roster's largest risk uses one epigraph calculation. These are standard linear optimization operations for the stated deployment representation. The instrument constraints of the earlier paper alone do not assert M03's operational fidelity or anchor-acquisition equivalence. [Balke and Pearl, original article](https://ftp.cs.ucla.edu/pub/stat_ser/r199-jasa.pdf)

Pearl and Bareinboim (2014), Definition 5 and Theorem 1, take two populations with compatible selection-diagram hypotheses. Theorem 1 gives a sufficient do-calculus reduction of P(y|do(x),z,s) to a form where selection variables appear only on the conditioning side of do-free terms, thereby identifying the target relation from allowed source and target information. That paper separately identifies prior necessity and a complete procedure for the interventional effect case. M03 assumes validated quantitative bounds and asks for an identified set or target threshold under those bounds. It does not discover causal arrows, do-calculus invariances or hidden support from audit margins. Selection diagrams might supply inputs in a specific case after the source and intervention conditions are checked. [Pearl and Bareinboim, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01603)

Bian and Abate (2017), Theorems 4–5, show for labeled Markov chains that ε-bisimilarity yields trace-law TV at finite depth bounded by 1−(1−ε)^k, tight across their class. This is an established full-trace comparison. T18 uses a common subprobability derived from verified source factors and trusted event labels. M03 can consume such a bound or pushforward as input and optimizes a specified event on correlated restricted law classes. Its event threshold can be decided even when full-trace equivalence is unknown. It does not improve the uniform trace TV bound or infer approximate bisimulation from similar observed marginals. [Bian and Abate, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.04547)

Iyengar (2005) establishes robust versions of dynamic programming results under rectangular conditional uncertainty, where admissible row selection factors in the appropriate dynamic sense. Goyal and Grand-Clément study structured nonrectangular uncertainty in transition factors and find robust policies under their factor-wise assumptions. Those works optimize controls under uncertain Markov dynamics. M03 fixes the observation-history policies or roster and uses a jointly represented full trajectory/occupancy law. It avoids splicing inconsistent marginals, but explicit trajectories can be exponentially large and general shared unknown transition kernels may induce nonpolyhedral constraints. This LP is a specific representational route, with no general robust policy synthesis or transferability of dynamic programs claimed. [Iyengar, original article](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.1040.0129) · [Goyal and Grand-Clément, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.00215)

## Minimum independently checked facts and finite calibration

Saikko, Wallner and Järvisalo (2016), Algorithm 1 and Lemma 2, use an implicit family of violated cores: an optimizer hits current cores, a checker either accepts the proposal or supplies a new core disjoint from its proposal, and finite termination gives an optimum when the two routines are exact. Ignatiev, Narodytska and Marques-Silva (2019) formulate related minimum explanations using feature assignments that force a specific predicted class under a given model. M03's opposing cores are realizable deployment-risk witnesses in a finite union of compact mass polytopes. The meaningful adaptation is that its certificate reads eligible implementation or calibration facts for a fixed independently defined event, with an actual threshold witness found by LP and separately checked membership. Once the oracle has been defined, the hitting-set logic follows directly from the prior general abstraction. Its prospective finite response tree is a different optimization that requires knowledge of admissible responses, and its use of primitive readback costs is domain-specific. [Saikko, Wallner and Järvisalo, full paper](https://wallner.sai.tugraz.at/files/SaikkoWJ2016.pdf) · [Ignatiev, Narodytska and Marques-Silva, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10656)

Hoeffding (1963), Theorem 2, gives the bounded independent sum tail inequality. The M03 interval guarantee applies both tails per registered cell and uses the union bound before any adaptive selection of which prevalidated batches to inspect. Coverage of probabilities is an existing ingredient. Constraining the operational target by those probabilities requires the independently justified finite episode representation and conditional event bridges. [Hoeffding, primary article](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

Overall judgment ADAPTATION. The combined finite framework is a concrete extension of the accepted PC-X mathematics. The proof gives no claim of general mathematical priority for compact risk image optimization, hitting-set duality, backward induction or finite simultaneous concentration.
