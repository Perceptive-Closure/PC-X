# M04 primary-source comparisons

## Extensive games and composite theory

Koller, Megiddo and von Stengel (1996), §2, show that in a two-player extensive game with perfect recall the sequence-form realization coordinates satisfy Ex=e, Fy=f. Expected terminal payoffs are bilinear with chance-weighted leaf contributions. Local realization ratios recover behavior. The paper dualizes best responses and treats equilibrium by a linear complementarity problem. M04.1 reuses the realization equations and dualizes one robust opponent best response for each constrained audit loss, with a shared auditor realization vector. It is an application of established sequence form and strong LP duality. The PC-X ingredients are a prespecified label for the inspected deployment, the real authorized information partitions, and the distinction among worst expected cost, support budget and class-specific failures in one feasibility region. [Koller, Megiddo and von Stengel, primary paper](https://ai.stanford.edu/~koller/Papers/Koller%2Bal%3AGEB96.pdf)

Sion (1958), Theorem 3.4, exchanges sup inf and inf sup for convex compact domains with the required quasiconcavity, quasiconvexity and one-sided semicontinuity. The M04.2 test vector and reweighted paired class laws give a continuous separately affine instance, so the exact weighted overlap formula follows by coordinatewise Bayes minimization. Huber and Strassen (1973) analyze robust Neyman–Pearson testing for alternating-capacity families, where representative least-favorable simple laws play a stronger role. M04.2 allows arbitrary compact convex families on a finite complete record alphabet and obtains a witness for a given pair of error caps. It claims no cap-independent universal least-favorable pair. The finite theorem is an adaptation of classical convex testing, with the entire stopped strategic law as the data object. [Sion, Theorem 3.4](https://msp.org/pjm/1958/8-1/pjm-v8-n1-p14-p.pdf) · [Huber and Strassen, original article](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2958011) · [Correction](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176342630)

Goldenshluger, Juditsky and Nemirovski (2015), Theorem 2.1, optimize over convex composite parameter sets under their stipulated observation scheme and derive likelihood-style detectors with Hellinger-affinity risk bounds. Their Lemma A.2 relates simple-testing overlap to near-optimality. M04 optimizes the exact class error caps for a known finite record law family via linear programming, with no requirement of product observations in that calculation. It does not derive their detector's sample-complexity improvements or efficient reduction from succinct model inputs. [Goldenshluger, Juditsky and Nemirovski, primary article](https://doi.org/10.1214/15-EJS1054)

## Sequential lower bounds and confidence

Kaufmann, Cappé and Garivier (2016), Lemma 1, lower-bound the sum of expected arm counts times KL by the binary divergence of a stopped event under two bandit models with mutually absolutely continuous arm laws and a.s. finite stopping. The accepted T22 adds the initial record and uses genuine conditional response KL on carried histories before applying its independent-reset specialization. M04.1 instead optimizes a finite authorized game including stopping, hidden state, hard path budget and separate label-specific failure constraints. It matches the optimum of its own exact finite objective and can give a strict gap above the existing change-of-measure relaxation. [Kaufmann, Cappé and Garivier, Lemma 1 and Appendix A](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume17/kaufman16a/kaufman16a.pdf)

Nitinawarat and Veeravalli, Theorem 5.1, derive first-order sequential asymptotics as risks tend to zero using controlled Markov observation kernels, nonuniform per-control costs and optimal cost-inferential information rates under their conditions. M04 excludes any analogous asymptotic information-rate claim for general nonanticipating nuisance and treats a bounded explicit tree exactly. M04.4 compares attainable finite-cap and infinite expected-cost frontiers under a positive per-probe floor by a uniform truncation bound and compactness. This statement preserves the chance of arbitrarily long supported paths and claims no efficient indefinite-horizon solver or new asymptotic KL bound. [Nitinawarat and Veeravalli, primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.1844)

Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon (2021) develop time-uniform confidence sequences via nonnegative supermartingales and provide concentration bounds for broad stochastic process conditions. M04.3 uses the accepted bounded-score conditional martingale and a direct telescoping error allocation over probe sample counts. The projected composite likelihood confidence set is a support-restricted likelihood ratio supermartingale construction. These are established coverage mechanisms. The actual audited class, admissibility of interval-overlap witnesses, and finite quotas under pairwise interval separation are model-specific conditions needed to recover T24. [Howard et al., primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08240v7)
