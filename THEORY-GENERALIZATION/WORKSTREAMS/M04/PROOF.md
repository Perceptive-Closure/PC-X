## Finite strategic design in actual information sets

Index terminal leaves by z. Each leaf carries its fixed deployment label, final declaration or abstention, and actual cost. For class k the leaf payoff is one if its initial label is k and its outcome is a wrong declaration or abstention, and zero otherwise. A hard path budget B uses the payoff 1{C(z)>B}. An expected cost cap uses payoff C(z). Imposing each class-specific loss cap against all responder policies gives the requested uniform requirement on that class because all root choices of the class can be exercised deterministically and the other-class leaves contribute zero. The label is an independent semantic property of the fixed deployment underlying each model. The auditor gets an informative initial record only when the authorized source supplies it.

For fixed deterministic auditor and responder contingent plans i and j the leaf probability is the product of its chance factors when all choices on the path match those plans, and zero otherwise. An auditor private seed yields a distribution over deterministic plans. Fix all private draws that might be read anywhere in the finite tree, then prescribe the resulting choice at each information set and complete unreached cases arbitrarily. The plan remains private. To account for common randomization or a visible cue, place its distribution and disclosure in the tree and let the plans react to observations actually made. The same argument encodes an opponent with an independent private seed. Its contingency table can carry and respond to history in its information sets. It cannot inspect a future or hidden opposing draw. The opponent may know the audit design.

Let p_i be the auditor plan probabilities. For a loss L let M^L_{ij} be its chance expectation under plan pair i,j. The robust constraints are

    p_i≥0,    Σ_i p_i=1,    Σ_i p_i M^L_{ij}≤b_L   for every j and L.

They are necessary since each pure j is admitted, and linearity makes them sufficient for each independent mixture, including the behavioral laws in the finite tree. Coupled restrictions can be represented by listing exactly the admitted plans and their mixtures. The nonnegative hard-budget violation indicator has mean zero if and only if no positive-probability leaf exceeds the budget. Expected cost independently bounds the mean. Declared stop choices and the information then available appear as tree nodes. To minimize worst expected cost or worst failure add a common variable upper bound for the corresponding rows. Compactness of the strategy simplex, closed feasibility and bounded finite-tree costs give attainment. When all input constants and caps are rational, exact solutions or infeasibility certificates are rational. The number of contingency plans can be exponential in the tree, namely the product of the action counts at its information sets.

### Sequence-form verification

For each player include the empty sequence, then a coordinate (I,a) for each own information-set/action pair. The information-set index distinguishes separate uses of a public action name. Perfect recall gives each I a unique sequence of the player's earlier information sets and chosen actions. Write σ(I) for the coordinate of its last preceding choice, or the empty sequence. A realization vector x has x_empty=1, x≥0, and Σ_a x_{(I,a)}=x_{σ(I)} for every I. Separate I sharing one predecessor each have their own flow row. These are Ex=e. Define Fy=f for the responder.

A distribution over pure plans induces x_s as the probability that its prescribed actions match the whole sequence. The proper choice events at I partition the event for its predecessor, giving the flow equalities. Conversely at I with positive predecessor weight, use action probabilities x_{(I,a)}/x_{σ(I)}. At a zero predecessor use any legal law. Induction on sequence length gives the same realization probabilities x. The analogous conclusion holds for y. Therefore the probability at z is c_z x_{s(z)} y_{t(z)}, with c_z the product of fixed chance probabilities and s(z),t(z) the last own sequence coordinates. The rule preserves all expected leaf losses simultaneously, using precisely the given information partitions and independent private choices.

For loss L_j, place c_z L_j(z) in A_j(s(z),t(z)) and add over leaves with those sequence pairs. The robust cap is

    max { (A_jᵀx)ᵀ y : Fy=f, y≥0 } ≤ b_j.

The opposing feasible polytope is nonempty and compact. Linear programming duality gives the same value from minimizing fᵀv_j subject to Fᵀv_j≥A_jᵀx with free v_j. One dual vector for each loss gives the single auditor LP. Each loss may attain its worst expectation at a different responder. The auditor x in all the constraints is common, so different loss witnesses cannot induce inconsistent auditor choices.

The tree has one sequence per action/information-set pair plus an empty coordinate, one flow equation per information set plus normalization, and at most one payoff contribution per leaf and loss. Filling matrices and multiplying rational probabilities incur polynomial input bit lengths, and solving the resulting rational LP and recovering local probabilities take polynomial time in the explicit input size. Histories may make the explicit tree exponentially larger than a concise physical dynamics description. Cross-information restrictions absent from the tree may invalidate the unrestricted responder dual. One must use an exact game encoding or the actual permissible plan list.

## Composite law criterion before the final declaration

For a fixed design with a complete pre-declaration record t, let φ(t) be probability of declaring PASS. The PASS error under P is Σ_t P(t)(1−φ(t)) and the FAIL error under Q is Σ_t Q(t)φ(t). Replacing raw class law families by their convex hulls preserves the extreme errors by linearity. When abstention counts as failure, replacing every abstention by PASS weakly improves the PASS error and leaves the FAIL error unchanged. Binary test feasibility therefore suffices.

Define the compact convex set K of pairs (u,v)=(λP,(1−λ)Q). Compactness follows by continuity on the product of compact sets. For convexity, at a weighted average of two pairs normalize the two nonzero subprobability vectors separately, giving a convex combination of probabilities from their own classes. Define

    g(φ,u,v) = Σ_t[u(t)(1−φ(t)) + v(t)φ(t)] − αΣ_tu(t) − βΣ_tv(t).

For fixed φ its maximum on K is the larger of the two worst class constraint excesses: each g is a weighted average of class excesses, and choosing λ=0 or 1 realizes the respective maximum. The minimum of this maximum over φ∈[0,1]^T is at most zero exactly when the test is feasible. The two compact convex domains and a continuous separately affine payoff allow minimax exchange. For fixed (u,v), the contribution before subtracting the two caps is a convex combination of u(t) and v(t) at every record. Minimize it by choosing the smaller. This proves the exact weighted overlap criterion, including inequality at equality and the strict-violation witness.

When each class is given by a finite list, write u=Σ_i p_iP_i and v=Σ_jq_jQ_j with nonnegative coefficients and Σ_i p_i+Σ_jq_j=1. Maximize Σ_t o_t−αΣ_i p_i−βΣ_jq_j subject to 0≤o_t≤u(t),v(t). At an optimum for fixed u,v, o_t is the minimum of its upper bounds. This is the claimed rational LP. Normalize nonzero groups to obtain the class mixtures at λ=Σ_i p_i. If the classes share a law, weight λ=1/2 gives overlap 1/2, hence α+β≥1 is necessary. For a fixed design, one strict witness obstructs every test on its allowed complete record; excluding every design requires establishing that obstruction for each design or using M04.1.

The T15 special case uses two admitted candidates with identical initial audit laws and equal conditional joint response rows where the genuine operations are jointly exercisable. Under every common adaptive protocol, multiplying the initial, query, response and stop terms gives equality of complete finite stopped records. The accepted measurable version instead integrates alternative regular conditional rows against a common preceding law on actual reached histories. Relevant private coins in the auditor record come from the same independent rule and only declared disclosures are available to the responder. T16's accepted bin-0 dispatcher varies the receiving card in the held-out installed registry of a fixed controller/schema. The deployment label varies because actual scoring compares deliveries to that card, while evaluation sees the other registry and its full records agree. One independently allowed read of the installed slot resolves the two labels. A fully specified identical deployment contract has the same risk under the same conditions.

## Recovering the accepted strategic interval theorem

Under an arbitrary actual admitted candidate c and nuisance, fix probe q. Let N_q(t) count its draws, S_q(t)=Σ_{i≤t:q_i=q}(Z_i−μ_i), and let μ_i be the conditional mean of the actual bit after predictable query q_i is chosen and before its response. Conditional Hoeffding gives E exp(λ(Z_i−μ_i))≤exp(λ²/8), with the expectation conditioned on the full pre-response audit record and query. This follows from the log moment generating function of a centered [0,1] variable, whose second derivative is a tilted variance at most 1/4 and whose value and first derivative at zero vanish. Thus

    L_q(t,λ)=exp(λS_q(t)−λ²N_q(t)/8)

is a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. The fact that a randomized query is selected before its response makes the multiplier one when a different query is selected and expectation at most one for q.

Let τ_n be the first time N_q=n. Stop at τ_n or at a finite deterministic cap. On the event τ_n is reached and S_q(τ_n)≥nu, the stopped process is at least exp(λnu−λ²n/8) for λ>0. Bound its expectation by one, set λ=4u and increase the cap. The negative λ argument proves the other tail. The probability of ever reaching count n with absolute centered average above u is at most 2exp(−2nu²). At the sealed b(n), summing over q,n spends at most δ because Σ_{n≥1}1/[n(n+1)]=1. The average corresponding true conditional mean is in the true interval, so the true candidate survives each sample-distance test on this simultaneous event. This uses no memory reset and allows all history-dependent nuisance respecting the genuine conditional means.

If two candidates survive a quota test for q, their respective intervals are each within b(n_q) of the sample average, hence the gap is at most 2b(n_q). A q separating a still-opposite pair has gap at least g_q>2b(n_q) and therefore cannot be at quota. Choose such a probe and take draws until its quota or a terminal set. The algorithm stops after at most Σ_q n_q draws under every response sequence; truth retention gives the correct nonempty homogeneous class except on the error event of size ≤δ. If there are no opposite labels it stops without sampling. Empty-set abstention counts as failure here. This is the explicit deterministic cap for the stated procedure.

Conversely, if an opposite pair's intervals overlap for every q, the richness and joint-record assumption supplies an admissible pair of stationary Bernoulli responders with common selected means and identical joint observed response rows and initialization. Their full laws under each permitted design agree. The T15 argument and the common-law obstruction rule out uniform failure <1/2. Separate marginals of any retained field cannot serve for this step. The clipped interval for honest score V follows from |E(Z|h,q)−E(V|h,q)|≤E(|Z−V| | h,q)≤η. Correlation between mismatch, prior record and honest score is permitted within that conditional bound.

For a capped score-only class with arbitrary permitted history-wise means, let the responder choose either interval endpoint at its relevant information set, then let chance give the specified Bernoulli bit. Any mean in the interval is a mixture of these endpoints. Hence private local mixtures implement each allowed conditional row. Conversely the auditor's conditional expectation of any such hidden endpoint mixture stays in the interval. In the score-only class the responder can use the public query/score history, with audit coins disclosed only through the recorded authorized choices. Under a fixed design, conditioning on an additional private auditor plan multiplies the probability of its public prefix by its own action factors; those same factors apply to each latent response prefix and cancel from their posterior given that public history. With the full set of unrestricted history-wise interval rows, the endpoint tree therefore realizes the same fixed-design complete record laws even if the auditor retains its private plan. Genuine hidden memory whose induced conditional score row is in the interval is covered. Further restrictions on correlations, access to private audit information, or extra output fields need an exact wider output encoding. The finite LP supplies the exact capped optimum for the explicit endpoint tree, while the concentration argument yields existence and an explicit cap without enumerating strategies under the broader sufficient T24 premises.

## Composite confidence and stopping

Keep the design fixed and pad every stopped record. Under any admitted actual law P a finite P-null record is never observed almost surely. On supported prefixes define Z_t=M(h_t)/P(h_t). Then

    E_P[Z_{t+1} | h_t]
      = Σ_{o:P(h_to)>0} M(h_to)/P(h_t)
      ≤ M(h_t)/P(h_t).

The countable times and consistency of M across children give a nonnegative supermartingale with initial mean one at the empty record. Stopping at the first crossing of 1/δ or a finite cap bounds the crossing probability by δ, then increasing the cap gives the simultaneous claim. Hence the same confidence rule protects each admitted actual law, even a continuum of mixture weights. Nonempty homogeneous issuance is correct on that event at any adapted stop. Empty sets can flag model-law incompatibility with the tests. An omitted law observationally identical to an admitted one cannot be detected from those records.

For a finite design with an explicit pure-opponent record list, every class law has mixture weights in a simplex. Keeping the law along a fixed path through time t imposes P(h_s)>δM(h_s) for a finite set of s≤t, including positive probabilities when M(h_s)=0. These are linear strict inequalities in the weights. Maximize a common slack, capped above by one, and accept a class as possible precisely when positive slack is feasible. This supplies an exact finite feasibility algorithm, with no proof of an efficient enumeration of extreme laws. The coverage statement allows abstention and controls only incorrect issuance. The independent-reset histogram stopping result from the baseline and the explicit interval-quota stopping result here require their separate sampling and separation conditions.

The T22 stopped KL decomposition still applies when its full-initialization, common protocol-factor, support and integrability requirements are satisfied. Informative initialization contributes its own KL term. It yields per-pair information lower bounds in that setting. M04.1 optimizes actual history-wise legal policies on the given finite tree and matches its own worst-cost or fixed-budget lower bound by construction. It need not attain a separate KL count relaxation or its large-sample asymptotic rate.

## Infinite-horizon closure under a positive per-probe price

For a feasible infinite rule let T be its stopping probe count. Follow the rule through H probes and replace any further continuation by a forced declaration. Coupling with the same chance and response laws through that prefix is possible by finite/infinite consistency and the absence of an informative change to earlier cues. The respondent may choose a different allowed plan after learning the public design. Every corresponding finite plan still extends to an allowed infinite plan, which the original uniform bound already covers. The capped cost is at most the original acquisition cost on each path. The changed behavior is confined to T>H, where the original cost is at least c_0(H+1). Markov's inequality bounds that event by B/[c_0(H+1)] for each responder, so at most that much failure is added in each class. A given feasible infinite bound B yields an explicit sufficient horizon for any specified positive error slack.

Conversely choose ε_n decreasing to zero and corresponding finite-cap policies meeting B,α+ε_n,β+ε_n. Embed them in the infinite tree, filling unused information sets arbitrarily after their finite terminal decisions. At each information set use behavior probabilities in a compact finite simplex. There are countably many information sets by finite branching. A diagonal subsequence converges at every information set to a legal behavioral rule. Against each fixed nonanticipating infinite responder, a finite prefix probability is a finite sum of products of the relevant finitely many auditor coordinates and fixed chance/response factors. This remains continuous for a fixed private mixture of responder plans since its probability of agreeing with each finite path is a constant coefficient. Every finite terminal failure probability and expected cost accumulated through H probes is therefore continuous in these coordinates. The limit inherits the α or β bound on each finite terminal failure event and the B bound for every finite cumulative cost. The event of indefinite continuation has infinitely many completed probes, so it entails infinite cost and has probability zero under that finite expected bound. Monotone convergence gives the required total expected cost and eventual failure probabilities. The same subsequence and limit policy work for every responder. Pointwise checking after taking this common limit preserves uniform bounds.

This proves an exact feasible-frontier characterization in the expected-cost metric. The limiting exact-error rule can take an unbounded number of probes on its support. Even the capped LP can grow with the full tree size, and no general efficient infinite-horizon decision algorithm follows.
