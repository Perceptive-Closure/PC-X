# M04 exact strategic fixtures and boundaries

## Opposite transcript-law classes with a common mixture

Use the fixed-implementation dispatch fixture with an independent receiving-card registry. The controller always dispatches a mandatory item to bin 0. The declared physical scoring rule compares the actual receiving bin to the installed deployment card. The authorized audit only reads an unrelated diagnostic bit. In two PASS completions the deployed card names bin 0. Their alternative diagnostic modes leave the same loaded bit constantly 0 or 1. In the FAIL completion the deployed card names bin 1 and the diagnostic bit is initialized fairly once per audit and remains in memory. All the physical action schemas and the target rule are fixed. The installation and diagnostic initialization are declared uncertain. No legal probe resets this stored bit or reads the receiving card.

For any fixed authorized adaptive design, the FAIL complete transcript law equals the 1/2–1/2 mixture of the two PASS complete laws. Couple the diagnostic initialization to the mixing bit and then use identical auditor coins and lawful responses, including through any finite retained stops or repeated readouts. Every design reaching a diagnostic read with positive probability has distinct FAIL and individual PASS laws, even though the convex class hulls overlap for all designs. For one compulsory read the simple-pair total variations are both 1/2. The worst uniform failure is at least 1/2, and a fair declaration attains it. Allowing an independent reset that generates a fresh bit on every read would be a different record class. Direct registry readback is an example of legitimate additional initialization that actually resolves the question.

## Disjoint hulls and an unequal least favorable class weight

A single two-symbol record has PASS law P=(1,0) and FAIL law Q=(1/3,2/3). Choosing FAIL on the second symbol is optimal. If the test chooses PASS with probability t on the first, its errors are 1−t and t/3. Their maximum is minimized at t=3/4 with value 1/4. The equal-prior Bayes error is only 1/6. The weighted overlap is max_λ min(λ,(1−λ)/3)=1/4 at λ=1/4. Therefore disjoint hulls and even an equal-weight optimum below the requested tolerance do not give arbitrary uniform caps. Per-symbol mixture weights are fixed for the whole law.

## Correlation in the complete record

Consider one scored bit Z and one authorized auxiliary field W, each marginally fair in both candidates. In one completion W=Z, in the other W=1−Z. The joint supports are disjoint. One draw resolves the target if both fields are legitimate and the target-to-correlation relationship is anchored. Interval overlap and matching marginal auxiliary laws would misclassify the information available. For a carried-state variant, the first response is fair and stored, then the second repeats it in one model and flips it in the other. The one-step output marginals agree and the complete law does not. Matching marginals or evaluating one-round fresh restarts loses that signal.

## Useful initialization, adaptive acquisition and information sets

A candidate has hidden label ℓ in {0,1} and a selector s in {0,1}. An authorized initial cue reveals s. The analyst has independently verified that legitimate probe q_s reports the corresponding installed slot's binary receiving designation ℓ and q_{1−s} returns an erasure. Each probe costs one and both are callable. With at most one query, the cue-adaptive choice q_s gives zero failure in all four worlds. If a protocol must choose its single query independently of the cue, at least one s has erasure probability at least 1/2. In that pair of worlds with common s, the only retained difference on erasure is the unknown label, so at least one has failure at least 1/4. Uniform query choice with a fair declaration on erasure attains 1/4. This compares a precise open-loop class, not a generally constrained adaptive design.

For private randomization, consider an authorized challenge whose hardware exposes a fixed deployment-linked response when the auditor's private bit and a responder's private guess differ, otherwise returning an erasure. The auditor's bit is sampled fairly and committed to the trusted instrument. The responder sees the challenge invocation but its guess is fixed before the bit's content could be known. Conditional on either label and any allowed guess the erasure probability is 1/2. The fair-guess completion and equal class prior give a lower bound of 1/4 for any design, and a fair declaration on erasure with the fair private challenge attains it. Giving the responder information about that auditor bit at guess time allows always forcing erasure and raises the optimum failure to 1/2. The trusted wiring and order are operational assumptions, not conclusions from public transcript frequencies.

## Costs, finite stopping and confidence

- **Expected versus hard cost.** With at most one unit-cost probe and two Bernoulli hypotheses p=1/3 versus p=2/3, the best equal-prior risk after a probe is 1/3. With no probe it is 1/2. If the independent sampling probability is a, any procedure has maximum class failure at least 1/2−a/6. Reporting from the observed bit and using a fair guess otherwise attains it for each class. At failure cap 2/5, the exact minimum worst expected cost is 3/5 and the exact minimum hard path cost is 1. The T22 count relaxation here is \((1/5)\log(3/2)/[(1/3)\log 2]\), strictly smaller than 3/5. At failure 1/3 its bound and the one-probe optimum both equal 1. This finite fixture gives a matched optimization objective when comparing lower and upper bounds.
- **No exact finite cap at zero error.** One unit-cost probe returns the known class label with probability 1/3, or an erasure otherwise, independently each round. Waiting until the first revealing reply stops a.s. with expected cost 3 and zero error. At any deterministic cap H the all-erasure record remains shared with mass (2/3)^H. The optimum symmetric failure is (2/3)^H/2, since the two records agree on erasures and one can guess fairly there. Positive costs and finite expected cost do not cap all supported paths.
- **Likelihood validity versus issuance.** In the accepted T23 counterexample, P₀ always yields a zero bit and P₁ is fair. Reference mixture weight 2⁻³³ on P₀ gives M/P₁=1+2⁻³³(2^t−1)<3/2 on 0^t through t=32. Both candidates survive a likelihood threshold 10. The distinct fixed histogram guarantee at δ=1/10, K=2, Δ=1/2 is decisive on its coverage event at n=32. The one valid stopping rule need not have the other's deadline.
- **Unenrolled law and untrusted score.** A completion outside the declared model family that gives a known law the same audit records escapes every record-only compatibility check on those paths. An unanchored scoring shift can therefore produce a selective favorable certificate even if the mathematical test is valid for admitted laws. The genuine target-to-score link and the worst-case histories for the mismatch bound require separate evidence.
