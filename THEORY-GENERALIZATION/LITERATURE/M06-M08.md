# Primary sources for M06–M08

## Belief control and actionable records

Chatterjee, Doyen, Henzinger and Raskin study observation-based strategies in finite games with imperfect information. Their knowledge construction tracks states compatible with the entire public history, and their antichain fixed point avoids explicit substitution of all subsets in the original graph. T29.J applies the same general principle, with immutable contract tags, a fixed verdict alongside actual work, observable branch prices and source-atomic authorization. It does not improve the general imperfect-information game bounds. [Chatterjee et al., Sections 2–3](https://lmcs.episciences.org/1094)

Kaelbling, Littman and Cassandra derive finite-horizon contingent plans from beliefs and actions in partially observable Markov models. These transition probabilities support expected reward rather than the sure worst-path objectives in T29.J and T30.CG. The latter's quotient therefore stores attained score extrema rather than a probability vector. Inferring its exactness from standard Bayesian sufficiency would leave the suffix-compatibility and threshold-attainment obligations unproved. [Kaelbling et al., Sections 3–4](https://people.smp.uq.edu.au/YoniNazarathy/Control4406_2014/resources/KaelblingLittmanCassandra1998.pdf)

## Decision diagrams and correlated scores

Bryant constructs ordered graph representations of Boolean functions and proves graph-size-dependent Boolean operations, including a paired construction for combining functions. This supplies a close antecedent for the paired path-compatibility calculation of T30.C. For T30.CG, the substantive specialized step is proving that a pair of attainable extrema at each edge preserves the relevant information for **all** dynamic meter and service histories, then using it in exact source-callable control with two kinds of costs. A general diagram solver or antichain algorithm can preserve useful correlations not covered by this particular serial reduction. Its relative performance needs a matched comparison. [Bryant, Sections 3–4](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emc/15414-s14/lecture/ieeetc86.pdf) · [Chatterjee et al., §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0706.2619)

## Point search and noisy calls

Grover's database-search formulation starts with a binary marked-location oracle and states the linear classical search requirement. T07.K retains this information model. It supplies a concrete governed semantics whose exceptional key changes real frozen service, a separate operational gateway target, an exact uniform randomized bound including both key and gateway, and matched expected noise costs. The result gives a domain-specific information separation from black-box search and does not purport to establish a new unstructured search lower-bound technique. [Grover](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605043)

Wald's sequential likelihood-ratio tests for two simple hypotheses provide the method for the two asymmetric random-walk barriers. T07.K combines per-key false-acceptance allocation with a common error allowance for the actual key. The resulting linear worst expected price at fixed noise and confidence is a mathematical bound for this declared query service. It supplies no stochastic time bound for an arbitrary responder. [Wald](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177731118)
