\begin{abstract}
How much must we understand about an AI system to justify a particular deployment decision? An evaluation can leave several explanations of the system's behavior open. If all give the same answer to the deployment question, the answer is identified. Acting on it requires a further step: one policy must deliver the promised service using only the observations and permissions available to it. We study these two requirements together. We characterize which trusted source checks settle a question, how much they cost, and which facts connect an evaluation to physical outcomes after a system learns its verdict. On one governed service, a single read settles feasibility while exact installation identification requires exponentially many point queries. For linked stochastic and strategic models we give exact synthesis results and, under verified approximation bounds, a certificate for the price of an implementable policy. Seventeen experimental packages examine the mechanisms and their limits. In prospective constructed services, useful work could supply a missing observation without a separate paid audit, and two separately implemented runs supported the declared risk bound among issued decisions. Some sharper comparisons remained inconclusive. Applying these results to a deployment requires independent evidence for its model, permissions and physical outcomes.
\end{abstract}
