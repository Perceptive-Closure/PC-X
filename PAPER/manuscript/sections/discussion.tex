\section{Implications for AI assurance}\label{sec:implications}
For a deployment decision, a useful audit can return three different artifacts: an opposed pair of still-possible installations, a receipt set that makes the property determinate, or a common policy with a verifiable performance bound. They answer different questions. A pair can show exactly which deployment fact the evaluation missed. A sufficient receipt set describes what an independent party must check. A common policy ties the certificate to work the controller can actually carry out. Reporting a failed search as an impossibility would lose these distinctions.

In the running example, a meaningful positive result would bind the admissible production routes to the real guard, establish the physical meaning of its grant, and show that an agent can still complete the promised engineering work using the receipts it receives. A new endpoint, a different finalizer or an additional information channel can change both cost and conclusion. The mathematical object worth preserving is therefore the decision \emph{with} its evidence, interface, scope and operational obligations.

\section{Relation to earlier work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Decision-specific information.}
Blackwell compared experiments through the decisions and risks they support \citep{blackwell}. Decision-region determination asks when tests justify one of a set of possibly overlapping decisions. Javdani et al.\ give an approximation guarantee for Bayesian expected test cost using hyperedge cutting \citep{javdani}. Generalized binary search chooses queries that separate hypotheses \citep{nowak}. Partial identification has a close analogue in reward learning: different reward functions can remain observationally equivalent while downstream objectives vary in their sensitivity to that ambiguity \citep{skalse}. Our disagreement tests and pricing inherit this logic. PC-X supplies a particular target derived from source-atomic interventions, prices its legitimate readouts, and separately requires one executable policy when work is promised. The exponential example is a point-query construction with a physically consequential key. It does not establish a new general lower bound for active learning.

\paragraph{Control and statistical testing.}
Controlled sensing studies how an observation choice changes the performance of sequential or fixed-size tests \citep{nitinawarat}. Time-uniform confidence sequences permit inference at data-dependent stopping times \citep{howard}. The finite controller representation uses the perfect-recall sequence form familiar from extensive games \citep{koller}. Iyengar gives robust dynamic programs under a rectangular uncertainty condition \citep{iyengar}; multiple-environment partially observable models allow the environment itself to remain fixed across a run \citep{bovy}. PC-X explicitly ties conditional source rows to one installation and responder, carries physical utility through the continuation, and prices checks supporting the presumed equations. The statistical inequalities, linear programs, convex separation arguments and decision recursions are established techniques. Their application here depends on the warranted source model.

\paragraph{Transport, verification and runtime assurance.}
Pearl and Bareinboim characterize transport of causal effects across populations using causal structure and source and target information \citep{pearl}. Our finite trajectory calculations instead start from declared, separately checked conditional row and event restrictions, then optimize the remaining risk and the facts needed to resolve a decision. Margrave verifies access-control policies and analyzes behavioral differences across versions \citep{fisler}. Simplex uses a verified safety controller in dynamic upgrades \citep{simplex}. Shielding restricts actions using a temporal-logic safety specification \citep{shield}. These systems motivate the importance of exact reachability and enforcement. The distinct question here is which parts of an uncertain installed guard and execution interface must be verified before applying a certificate, with useful work and acquisition cost included.

\paragraph{Strategic AI and oversight.}
AI Control evaluates deployment protocols for safety and usefulness in a red-team setting \citep{control}. Games for AI Control formalizes the protocol-designer and adversary interaction and analyzes safety--usefulness tradeoffs \citep{games}. Experiments on alignment faking illustrate that behavior can depend on perceived training conditions in a constructed language-model setting \citep{faking}. Debate proposes using competing agents to help a human judge difficult claims \citep{debate}. PC-X asks what full pre-verdict record laws can distinguish, and what independently inspectable mechanism keeps the same responder's later action within a physical bound. This offers a way to formulate the evidentiary assumptions of an oversight protocol. It provides no general correspondence from a debate verdict or evaluation score to a deployed system's behavior.

The most specific contribution across these comparisons is a single account in which a source fact can exclude a competing explanation, certify a command, price the evidence, and make real work feasible. The exact separations, joint obstruction witnesses and cost frontiers are proved within the stated families. We make no general priority claim for property-specific knowledge, robust planning or strategic testing.

\section{Limitations and open problems}\label{sec:limits}
A correct proof over a class that omits the deployed installation can be perfectly misleading about that installation. Source membership, the coverage of the action inventory, the interpretation of signed records and the true-event decoder require independent evidence. A sound outer approximation can support a conservative feasible policy. Failure in that approximation only refutes deployment when its obstruction is physically realizable. A confidence level attached to sampling cannot price the chance that an unaudited premise is false.

Several computational costs lie outside the headline acquisition prices: obtaining and maintaining the source correspondence, compiling a finite record tree, solving a hard optimization problem, implementing randomization, and supplying the work itself. Compact graphs require special structure. Expected prices and supported-path caps answer different operational questions. The bounded responder classes preserve memory and verdict dependence without encompassing arbitrary strategic model behavior. Uniform infinite-horizon guarantees require additional tail, fallback and observability conditions (Theorem~\ref{adv:ug4}).

The experiments use authored source laws, bounded adapters, named scoring functions and shared infrastructure. Their independent reproductions check calculations and executions within these constraints. They are not independent field deployments of frontier systems. Extending the theory to externally operated interfaces with incomplete source access, measured costs of trustworthy instrumentation and longer-lived adaptive behavior remains open. The original D-35 manuscript and 43-page theorem charter were recovered; a distinct earlier extension charter was unavailable. The corrected mathematical packages determine the scope of the results here.

\section{Conclusion}
A rehearsal can fail to expose a missing guard even if it is repeated indefinitely. Conversely, one independently trustworthy read can be enough to establish the relevant guarantee while leaving many operationally distinct systems possible. PC-X makes that difference testable. It identifies which contrary installations survive, prices observations that remove them, and constructs a policy when the remaining possibilities support real work. The strength of a deployment decision depends on the property, the evidence and the actions that the verified interface actually supports.
