\section{Questions for frontier AI assurance}\label{sec:implications}
Consider the decision to let an agent perform a bounded task with access to a consequential tool. Before deployment, specify the forbidden physical outcome, the work owed, the receipt that reaches the controller, and the alternatives still consistent with the evidence. The mathematics then suggests concrete investigations.

\paragraph{Evaluation and scalable oversight.}
Which features of a strategic evaluation would survive into deployment after the model learns its verdict? In the setting of Theorem~\ref{body:strategy}, two classes sharing a complete audit-law mixture defeat uniform classification even though their individual members can look different. A researcher can specify which additional observation would break the overlap, then test the timing and independence assumptions it needs. In scalable oversight the corresponding question is which much smaller target a judge could verify. The gateway theorem supplies one implementable family and an explicit price comparison. Establishing such a separation on a new oversight protocol requires its actual query and work interfaces.

\paragraph{Interpretability and monitoring.}
A mechanistic observation becomes decision-relevant when it rules out an explanation that changes the target or blocks a common policy. In the release example two possible decoders can emit identical green receipts while only one binds the grant to the executed command. An interpretable internal feature could become an anchor if its relation to that command and the counterfactual is independently established. For a monitor, measuring alarms alone leaves missed physical violations unknown. The joint monitor/event analysis in Section~\ref{sec:verify} identifies what calibration would have to observe.

\paragraph{AI Control and agentic deployment.}
A control protocol may route suspicious actions for human approval and allow other work to proceed. Which trusted inspection establishes that every admitted route obeys its decision? Which loss of service follows from refusing all uncertain requests? The safeguard theorem prices existing verifiable answers to the first question. Joint synthesis asks whether one observable policy achieves the specified safety and useful-work floors together. The constructive examples give a design and a testable set of premises. Operating them on a frontier system would require independent access to its source boundaries, a defensible responder class, physical scoring and an account of routes outside the model.

\section{Relation to earlier work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Knowing enough to decide.}
Statistical decision theory already compares information by the risks it makes attainable \citep{blackwell}. Manski bounds treatment effects while allowing unobserved potential outcomes to vary \citep{manski}; reward-learning analyses distinguish rewards that remain observationally equivalent and downstream objectives sensitive to that equivalence \citep{skalse}. Javdani et al.\ give a close constructive precedent: choose paid tests until the hypotheses consistent with their outcomes fit in one of several possibly overlapping decision regions; their hyperedge algorithm approximates Bayesian expected cost \citep{javdani}. Generalized binary search separates hypotheses through queries \citep{nowak}. Controlled sensing studies how chosen observations affect error, including asymptotic testing exponents \citep{nitinawarat}. These approaches already establish the value of decision-specific information. The PC-X acquisition questions introduce a target derived from whole authorized operations, distinguish a realized receipt from a worst-case audit, and require a common physical policy when service is promised. Point search, weighted hitting sets and the minimax recursion are established tools. T07.K contributes the operational family carrying the priced separation.

\paragraph{Risk, control and verification.}
An extensive game's sequence form already represents randomized behavior with perfect recall \citep{koller}. Robust dynamic programming admits particular independence conditions on uncertain transition rows \citep{iyengar}. Multi-environment partially observable Markov decision processes already seek one policy maximizing worst reward when a hidden environment is fixed throughout the run \citep{bovy}. Recent complexity results depend on the model subclass and allowed policy class \citep{bovycomplex}. Parametric probabilistic verification already synthesizes fixed-size controllers for partially observable systems through properties of induced parametric Markov chains \citep{junges}. Time-uniform confidence sequences already support valid inference at supported stopping times \citep{howard}. PC-X uses those distinctions to retain one unknown installation, charge authorized reads, and enforce exact permissions alongside an issued-case denominator and actual work. Its algebraic decision result and counted-state construction require the stated representations. None implies tractability for a general frontier system.

\paragraph{Transport and enforcement.}
Causal transportability determines when source experiments and target observations identify a causal effect under an explicit model of population differences \citep{pearl}. Approximate probabilistic bisimulation already yields finite-horizon total-variation bounds on trace laws of labeled Markov chains \citep{bian}. Total variation is the largest difference the two laws give any event. PC-X takes verified controller, event and conditional source correspondences as inputs, bounds the remaining risk, and can price the least additional anchor facts that resolve the decision. Access-control verification analyzes policy changes \citep{fisler}; Simplex uses a verified controller for runtime safety \citep{simplex}; shielding restricts actions to enforce a specified temporal property \citep{shield}. These supply candidate mechanisms. The mathematical burden here includes which installed mechanisms must actually be present, how that fact can be learned, and whether useful work survives the resulting restrictions.

\paragraph{Strategic evaluation and human oversight.}
AI Control evaluates safety and usefulness against an intentionally subversive system \citep{control}. Games for AI Control models a designer and adversary in a partially observable stochastic game and studies deployment-policy tradeoffs \citep{games}. Alignment-faking experiments supply a concrete setting in which behavior responds to perceived training conditions \citep{faking}. Debate proposes training competing agents whose claims a human judges \citep{debate}. For such protocols PC-X poses an additional question: can the evaluator's complete accessible record distinguish the property being certified, and which trusted physical fact would change the answer? A.G1 uses classical minimax testing; A.G2 uses convex intersection and hitting sets. Their source-specific content is the common design across all worlds, preserved guard, surviving opposite reference cases, and the cost of an evidenced, usable decision. We have not established priority for these general mathematical methods or compared all possible assurance frameworks.

\section{Limitations and open problems}\label{sec:limits}
A deployment guarantee requires evidence that the actual installation belongs to the modeled class, that the listed commands cover its interface, and that signed records describe the true event. A sound outer model can certify a feasible policy. Its infeasibility refutes deployment only with a physically realizable obstruction. Sampling confidence cannot quantify the chance of an unaudited premise being false.

Acquisition prices exclude building and maintaining the source correspondence, compiling and solving the policy model, implementing its lotteries and doing the work. Compact graphs require structure. The bounded responders carry memory and react to a verdict within a specified program class. Uniform infinite-horizon claims require additional tail, fallback and observability conditions (Theorem~\ref{adv:ug4}).

The experiments share infrastructure and use authored laws, bounded interfaces and specified physical scores. Independent replay checks those implementations. External frontier deployments, sparse access to ground truth and longer-lived adaptation remain open. The recovered D-35 paper, the 43-page charter and the corrected governing packages fix the provenance of this account. A distinct early extension charter was unavailable.

\section{Conclusion}
How much must we understand before entrusting an intelligent system with consequential work? A particular guarantee can be known while the system remains unidentified. When explanations disagree, a counterexample can show exactly which unknown matters. When legitimate checks suffice, the theory can characterize them and, on the stated interfaces, optimize their acquisition.

A deployment decision also needs a verified connection to the physical process, including what can happen after the system learns its verdict. One controller must deliver the promised work across the possibilities left open. In some models that is impossible. In others, useful work itself supplies the missing evidence.

For a frontier system the research task is to establish these premises at its actual interfaces and test the decision against credible alternatives. The aim is to determine what must be understood, what may remain unknown, and how sufficient knowledge can justify trust and useful action.
