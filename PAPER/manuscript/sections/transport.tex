\section{When does evaluation say something about deployment?}\label{sec:transport}
A staging guard and a production guard might print the same receipt and operate on different endpoints. To transfer a risk bound one needs a correspondence for the commands the controller can actually call, their complete observations, and the physical event being scored. An \emph{anchor} is an independently verified source fact supplying part of that correspondence, such as a binding to a finalizer or a justified bound on a changed conditional outcome law.

\begin{theorem}[Risk from checked rows and event meanings, generalized T18]\label{body:transport}
Fix finitely many deployment contexts and protocols with specified lawful policies and a common physical violation event. Suppose admissible joint masses of context, trajectory and event, across all declared runs, are given by finitely many nonempty bounded linear models, one per admitted semantic mode. Checked bounds on differences of conditional rows and on conditional event rates may be included. For one specified run in each mode, the smallest and largest attainable violation probabilities are the minimum and maximum of a linear objective. For the worst risk across runs, minimize a common upper bound on their risks or take the largest of their separate maxima. The union of the resulting intervals is the exact scalar risk image when the masses describe precisely the realizable installations. Under outer inclusion it bounds that image. Appendix~\ref{app:extensions}, Theorems~\ref{ext:m031}--\ref{ext:m032}, specifies the full representation and the minimum source evidence for a threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Why the calculation works]
Write \(q_{xyb}\) for deployment mass in context \(x\), complete outcome cell \(y\) and true event bit \(b\). The risk is \(\sum_{x,y}q_{xy1}\). A conditional upper bound \(u_{xy}\) becomes \(q_{xy1}\le u_{xy}(q_{xy0}+q_{xy1})\). A bound on row distance is multiplied by the context mass and absolute differences are represented by auxiliary variables. This avoids division by zero. The resulting feasible region for one mode is compact and convex, so its scalar image is the full interval between its attained endpoints. Shared facts are constraints on the same arrays. Optimizing each cell independently can enlarge the answer. For a realized source report, a subset suffices precisely when it excludes every physically represented opposing completion. This is the certificate condition of Section~\ref{sec:id}, now applied to source facts about transport.
\end{proof}
For a simple illustration, if a shared lawful path and its physical event match on mass \(0.9\) while the rest is unrestricted, a bounded binary risk can change by up to \(0.1\). Even infinitely many tests of the matched paths say nothing about the remaining mass without a bridge. Such a bound applies only to the events and commands in the correspondence. An omitted production route can invalidate the membership premise.

With unknown but fixed probabilities one can retain a single installed parameter across all draws (Appendices~\ref{app:flagship} and~\ref{app:probability}). Replacing it by independent choices at successive rows changes the set of possible laws. If two bits are independent conditional on one fixed, unknown success probability \(\theta\), their chance of differing is \(2\theta(1-\theta)\), at most \(1/2\). Independently choosing success probabilities zero and one would give one. Sampling can narrow a numerical interval when the instrument and reset laws are established. It cannot establish command authority. This distinction matters particularly when a certificate is issued on a small fraction of attempts.
