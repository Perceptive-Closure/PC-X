\section{What carries an evaluation result into deployment?}\label{sec:transport}
A staging guard and a production guard can print the same receipt and operate on different endpoints. If the only evidence describes staging, one compatible production installation can admit a route that another rejects. More staging observations, even an exact staging law, cannot settle their difference. An \emph{anchor} is an independently established fact that connects the two processes. It might identify the finalizer actually reached by a command, the physical event behind a receipt, or a bound on the change in a conditional probability.

\paragraph{How much of a run can we match?}
Fix a finite horizon and a policy that chooses the same distribution of callable commands at matched observable histories. Here a conditional row gives the distribution of the next outcome given the preceding history and command. Check the initial state maps, authority, source effects and the physical meaning of the event separately. At every represented checkpoint suppose the same verified mapping decodes primitive outcomes into the next state, public observation and true event. Suppose the initial laws and successive conditional draws admit verified common lower masses on their matched prefixes. Multiplying the lower masses along each path gives a measure \(\mu\) dominated by both true path laws. Its total mass may be less than one. Write \(V=1\) for an actual violation and \(V=0\) for its absence. Both violation probabilities lie in
\[
 [\,\mu(V=1),\;1-\mu(V=0)\,].
\]
The left endpoint counts violations already paired, and the upper endpoint charges every remaining outcome as a possible violation. If the checked initial and conditional laws and event semantics agree exactly, the entire decoded path laws agree under this policy. If matched paths have mass at least \(0.9\), two binary risks can differ by at most \(0.1\) (Figure~\ref{fig:transport}). Unmatched paths still require lawful commands. Even a tiny unmatched mass can carry a forbidden action. Theorem~\ref{thm:t18} proves this by induction using both systems' conditional rows, including the initialization.

\begin{theorem}[Deployment risk and the price of its anchors, generalized T18]\label{body:transport}
Fix finitely many contexts, runs and semantic modes. Each run fixes a lawful policy, horizon and true event. In each inhabited mode the allowed joint masses of contexts, trajectories and events form a bounded, closed linear model. Include checked conditional-row distances, conditional event rates and all shared constraints. For the maximum risk over the named runs, the possible values in each mode form an attained interval. Linear programs compute both endpoints. Their union across modes is the exact risk image if the arrays describe precisely the realizable installations. A verified outer model gives conservative bounds. Given a consistent report and finitely many jointly available, nondisturbing anchor reads of nonnegative price, the least price certifying a threshold is the minimum price that excludes every opposing completion. Theorems~\ref{ext:m031} and~\ref{ext:m032} give the source and prospective versions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Why the two calculations fit together]
For a named run write \(q_{xyb}\) for mass in context \(x\), trajectory cell \(y\) and event value \(b\). Its risk is \(\sum_{x,y}q_{xy1}\). A conditional rate cap \(u_{xy}\) becomes \(q_{xy1}\le u_{xy}(q_{xy0}+q_{xy1})\), valid even at zero mass. A row-distance bound limits total variation, the largest probability difference over events. Multiplying by common context mass expresses that bound as linear restrictions with auxiliary variables for absolute differences. The maximum of the run risks is continuous on the compact convex model, hence takes every value between its extrema. Minimize a variable bounding every run risk from above for the lower endpoint; maximize each run risk and take the largest for the upper endpoint. For the audit, an opposing completion survives exactly when all selected facts hold there. Section~\ref{sec:acq}'s disagreement search therefore finds the cheapest sufficient facts with a complete separation oracle.
\end{proof}
For example, if a router read identifies the production endpoint but two possible finalizers at that endpoint decode a green receipt differently, that read alone cannot certify the risk. An authenticated check of the decoder can be essential. Compilers still compatible with both checks can be left unresolved if they give the same threshold verdict. At a realized report we pay for the retained checks. To promise a price before inspecting an installation we need all possible joint reports, keep the installed report fixed throughout, and price the worst possible branch of the adaptive audit.

\subsection{One policy for a continuously uncertain installation}\label{body:continuous}
A parameter can affect several transitions even when its numerical value is unknown. Consider two coin draws independent \emph{conditional on one installed bias} \(\theta\in[0,1]\). The chance they differ is \(2\theta(1-\theta)\), at most \(1/2\), attained at \(\theta=1/2\). Choosing bias zero for the first draw and one for the second would give probability one. That pair of rows describes no installation in the stated family.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-parameter robust synthesis, P.G01.1]\label{body:algebraic}
Take a finite-horizon, finite record-and-command tree with perfect recall and independent private policy randomness. Each world has a finite regime and one fixed parameter vector. Its admitted region, complete normalized conditional laws, intrinsic labels and bounded score graphs are \emph{rational semialgebraic}: finite Boolean descriptions by polynomial equations and inequalities with rational coefficients. Any denominators are certified positive. The implementation supplies the stipulated observations, actions, full support and true scores. Designate a rational-semialgebraic guard class \(W\) and a nonempty rational-semialgebraic performance class within it. Require exact supported-path legality throughout \(W\) and finitely many closed affine bounds in the policy variables at each performance world. Existence of one common policy is decidable. A feasible policy can be represented by real algebraic numbers. If the policy polytope is nonempty and has affine dimension \(d\), infeasibility is witnessed by at most \(d+1\) admissible algebraic worlds and their applicable constraints. See Theorem~\ref{adv:g011}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[The source of the decision procedure]
Represent the controller by the fractions of its private random choices assigned to each observable decision sequence. The fractions are nonnegative, start at one and split among available actions. They form a compact polytope \(X\), a bounded solution set of linear equalities and inequalities. For a fixed world, multiply a leaf's policy fraction by all its chance probabilities, using that \emph{same} parameter along the entire leaf. Summing scored leaves is affine in the fractions and semialgebraic in the joint variables. A nonnegative marker with mean zero forbids every supported hard breach, including one confined to an isolated parameter. The question now has the form
\[
 \exists x\in X\quad\forall\text{ applicable worlds }w:\quad
               x\text{ meets its requirements in }w .
\]
Quantifier elimination over the reals decides this expression. Compactness yields a finite subsystem if the closed constraints have empty intersection. Helly's theorem in affine dimension \(d\) reduces the obstruction to \(d+1\) worlds. Algebraic sampling yields witnesses; a real algebraic number is a real root of a nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients.
\end{proof}
Thus infinitely many possible dynamics can admit one implementable decision rule under the stipulated randomness model. Minimizing bounded worst expected cost, or maximizing guaranteed expected utility, on a nonempty robust policy set attains its optimum even when a world's extreme value is only approached. The finite witness concerns infeasibility. Describing the entire feasible region or its optimum can require infinitely many constraints. Physical counterexamples require realizable worlds, an exact physical optimum requires coverage of the competing policies, and realizing an algebraic lottery can itself require resources. The decision procedure can be expensive even on small horizons.
