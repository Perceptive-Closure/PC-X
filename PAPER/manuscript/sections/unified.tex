\section{One installation from observation to actual work}\label{sec:unified}
Can one affordable policy answer the right question, remain safe after its verdict is disclosed and deliver something useful? The following construction puts these obligations on the same run. It compares policies on the same installed source, with each comparison's observation menu stated explicitly.

A real parameter \(\theta\) lies in either \([1/8,1/4]\) or \([3/4,7/8]\). Its value is the risk of a separately specified ordinary operation. The intrinsic target is positive on the first interval, where ordinary risk is below \(1/2\). The proposed \emph{delegated} service has its own risk and usefulness requirements. It first receives a sequence of \(N\ge1\) bits from a persistent responder and completes a safe inventory job. This job delivers the actual route token. At that route it may pay \(3/8\) for a low-quality observation, pay one for a high-quality observation, or skip. The high observation is a bit with success probability \(\theta\); the low one has success probability \(1/4+\theta/2\), conditional on the entire past. Issuing a positive decision starts actual finishing work. The response program sees the decision and uses its retained state to select a proposal before the finalizer makes its fresh draw.

An installation check verifies which guard revision is in use. The good revision permits a surcharge \(\beta\) on the finalizer's conditional violation probability with \(0\le\beta\le1/32\); the bad revision has \(\beta=1/2\). The check authenticates this connection to the real mechanism. It does not reveal \(\theta\). The event \(I\) is both positive issuance and actual activation. In this construction \(G\) gives half credit for completed intake and half more for safe successful finishing on the positive installations. Appendix~\ref{app:unified} gives the complete scoring rule and the carried-state dynamics.

\begin{theorem}[A common governed policy with exact cost, U.G3]\label{body:unified}
On the verified good branch of this base interface, require uniform binary-decision error at most \(1/3\), selective violation risk at most \(3/8\), positive issuance at least \(2/3\) on positive installations, and expected utility of at least \(1/2\) everywhere and \(25/32\) on positives. The minimum worst expected per-attempt acquisition is \(7/12\) and the minimum hard acquisition cap is one. The best high-or-skip policy costs \(2/3\) in expectation. A good guard report costs \(1/4\) once per installation and is a least realized sufficient setup in the stated catalog. One policy gives selective risk at most \(5/16\) everywhere and \(5/32\) on positives, issues with probability at least \(1/4\) everywhere, and has expected utility at least \(25/32\) on positives.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[The price is forced by two indistinguishable starts]
The complete pre-probe record law agrees at \(\theta=1/4\) and \(\theta=3/4\) with other installed settings matched. Let \(l,h\) be the probabilities of buying low or high on this pair. They are identical on the two worlds and \(l+h\le1\). The largest difference in positive declaration probabilities is \(l/4+h/2\), the weighted differences between the two bit laws. Errors at most \(1/3\) require a difference of at least \(1/3\). Hence
\[
 (3/8)l+h=(5/8)(l+2h)-(l+h)/4\ \ge\ 7/12 .
\]
With high or skip alone, \(h\ge2/3\). If the hard cap is below one then \(h=0\) and the required separation is impossible. To attain the bound, buy low with probability \(2/3\) and high with probability \(1/3\), and issue when the observed bit is zero. The issue probability is \(5/6-2\theta/3\), giving the claimed errors and exposure. The choice of quality can be drawn before the token arrives; the actual command still requires its route. On issuance the finalizer's violation probability is \(\theta/4+a/16+\beta\), where the carried proposal \(a\) is zero or one. Applying this bound at every reached cut gives the selective bounds. On a positive world the expected utility is at least \(1/2+(1/2)(2/3)(1-5/32)=25/32\). An unchecked bad revision can survive the cheaper ordinary-route inspection and violates the required cap on a positive world. The guard report excludes it.
\end{proof}

The nominal finalizer uses zero surcharge. Coupling it to the actual one with the same uniform word, the same carried proposal, and the same preceding record gives an exact increase \(\beta\Pr(I)\) in violation mass. Applicable numerical error does not relax a hard permission. A richer interface can beat the price, and a missing guard can invalidate the selective certificate. The measured example in the next section tests both limits.
