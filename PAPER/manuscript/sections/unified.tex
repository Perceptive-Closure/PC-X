\section{One policy from evidence to useful action}\label{sec:unified}
A laboratory can optimize a test and an engineer can optimize a controller, yet their choices can be incompatible. A test may withhold the route the controller needs or disclose the verdict that changes a responder's next move. Joint synthesis asks whether \emph{one} observable policy can gather evidence, decide, and act in the installation that generated its evidence. Three results describe how to compute and represent it. We then give a service on which their premises can be checked.

\subsection{Which combinations of guarantees can one policy attain?}\label{body:joint}
Fix one installed world \(w\) and a controller. Write \(\Pr_w\) and \(\mathbb E_w\) for the resulting probability and expectation. Let \(I\) be actual positive issuance, \(V\) a true violation, \(G\) genuine service utility and \(K\) the acquisition charge along the run. The monitored record and the physical scoring record may be different. Which action follows issuance belongs to the physical specification. Whole-run risk is \(\Pr_w(V)\); risk \emph{among issued cases} is \(\Pr_w(V\cap I)/\Pr_w(I)\) when issuance has positive probability.

\begin{theorem}[Joint governed synthesis, T30.U.1]\label{body:jointthm}
Take finitely many complete worlds with finite horizons, fixed target labels, finite observation and action alphabets, and rational initial and transition probabilities. Include every relevant responder state, support, price, true event and service outcome. The controller remembers its actions and received records and may use independent private randomness. All calls and stops must be legal on their whole reachable supports. For rational bounded additive score data the simultaneously attainable vectors of expected scores, one coordinate per score and world, form a rational polytope. A linear program finds a common controller minimizing worst expected acquisition subject to worldwise affine constraints on errors, issuance, risk, utility and cost (Theorem~\ref{flag:u1}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[What the variables represent]
The policy variables record the fractions of the controller's private random choices that select a sequence of actions at the observed records. A fixed-world terminal probability is the product of that sequence fraction and the world's chance factors. Summing the scores gives a linear image of the same compact set of policy variables for every world. Recover action probabilities by dividing each child's fraction by the parent's at reached decisions. To require selective risk at most \(\varepsilon\), impose
\[
 \Pr_w(V\cap I)-\varepsilon\Pr_w(I)\le0,\qquad
 \Pr_w(I)\ge a_w>0
\]
wherever the guarantee is required. The second inequality ensures real exposure. Expected useful work conditional on issuance is handled in the same way with the actual work score. For a supported-path prohibition or hard price cap, give each offending reachable path a nonnegative marker and require mean zero in every guarded world.
\end{proof}
The price objective is \(\sup_w\mathbb E_wK\); a hard cap controls each path even if rare. Numerical risk allowances cannot waive a permission. A feasible formal solution transfers to an installation with a sound implementation and verified membership. An actual optimum or impossibility also needs coverage of all competing physical policies and the required worlds. The finite program is measured in the size of the expanded decision tree.

\subsection{When can a controller forget the order of its observations?}\label{body:states}
Two records can be merged if a controller receiving either of them has the same future opportunities for the quantities being scored. Merely having the same most likely world is insufficient: possible rare events may change a hard permission.
\begin{theorem}[Two sufficient-state representations, T30.U.2 and U.G1]\label{body:representation}
In the finite model, give each listed world a strictly positive \emph{reference} probability. Update the distribution of (world, hidden state) by the likelihood of the received data under the actions actually imposed. Keep this distribution, time and the observed public register. If they determine the relevant future laws, permissions and scores, policies on this state attain every full-history performance vector simultaneously in every world (Theorem~\ref{flag:u2}).

A separate prior-free result allows continuously varying parameters. Assume finitely many world types, each with a fixed hidden start, a compact rational semialgebraic parameter region and a finite hidden state. The next public state is determined by time, current public state, action and observed letter. For each type, the next hidden state is also determined by its previous state and these observed inputs, independently of the parameter. Each letter likelihood belongs to a finite list of normalized-row, nonnegative polynomial factors for that type. Retain time, public register, each type's propagated state and its counts of the factors used. If this record determines common legality, stops, supported-path restrictions and conditional score means in each surviving world, flows on these counted states attain exactly the full-history vectors (Theorem~\ref{adv:ug1}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Why each world keeps its own performance]
In the finite version, average the historical action choices at each common state using their occupation probabilities under the reference distribution. Induction preserves the joint world, state and action probabilities. Divide each world's contribution by its strictly positive starting weight to recover its own performance. Those weights are a bookkeeping device and impose no deployment frequencies. In the counted version, a fixed world's record likelihood is a product of its factors, one for each occurrence. Equal counts give equal products. Aggregate the controller's own sequence fractions at the merged state, counting each incoming observation edge even if two letters share a destination. Multiplying these flows by the fixed world's likelihood recovers its genuine probability and expected scores. Normalizing outgoing flows recovers a common randomized policy.
\end{proof}
This equality preserves worldwise probabilities of the represented events and the legal actions, strategic memory, actual risk, utility and charges needed for the decision. Individual old words need not be reconstructible. Unrecorded timing, order-sensitive responder memory or a newly callable command can invalidate a merge.

\subsection{How close is an implementable policy to the physical optimum?}\label{body:interval}
A controller verified on a grid of reference models may still miss an actual world between grid points. To certify an answer one needs both a lower bound applying to every physical competitor and a feasible policy whose price can actually be attained.
\begin{theorem}[A certified price interval, U.G2]\label{body:certified}
Let \(A^*_{\mathrm{actual}}\) be the least worst expected acquisition over an exhaustive common compact rational policy polytope encoding exact physical permissions. Divide actual worlds into finitely many compact labeled strata. In each stratum use a finite grid of actual worlds, with every actual world within known distance \(r\) of a grid point. For each affine performance constraint and the bounded cost, suppose the reference score changes by at most \(M\) times world distance and differs from its actual counterpart by at most \(e\), uniformly over policies. The constants may differ by score and stratum. With exact coefficients or certified one-sided bounds, the relaxed and tightened grid problems give, when both are feasible,
\[
                     L\le A^*_{\mathrm{actual}}\le U .
\]
The upper solution supplies an implementable policy with cost at most \(U\). Theorem~\ref{adv:ug2} also bounds the gap under an explicit strict-margin condition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an actual cap \(b\), every actual feasible policy passes the reference cap \(b+e\) at grid points. Subtract the cost discrepancy from the reference price there to obtain the lower minimum \(L\). For the upper problem impose the reference cap \(b-e-Mr\). Moving to the nearest grid point costs at most \(Mr\), and moving from reference to actual costs at most \(e\). Add the corresponding two cost allowances to the reference price to get \(U\). Lower infeasibility rules out an actual policy. Upper infeasibility can simply mean insufficient margin on this grid.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A guarded service that realizes the requirements together}\label{body:guarded}
The following is a specific source, distinct from the earlier two-job gateway and three-installation examples. It supplies a checkable answer to the release team's question. A work receipt identifies the callable route, a paid observation informs a verdict, and a checked finalizer constrains what can happen after the responder sees that verdict.

A real parameter \(\theta\) lies in either \([1/8,1/4]\) or \([3/4,7/8]\). Its value is the risk of a separately specified ordinary operation. The intrinsic target is positive on the first interval, where ordinary risk is below \(1/2\). The proposed \emph{delegated} service has its own risk and usefulness requirements. It first receives a sequence of \(N\ge1\) bits from a persistent responder and completes a safe inventory job. This job delivers the actual route token. At that route it may pay \(3/8\) for a low-quality observation, pay one for a high-quality observation, or skip. The high observation is a bit with success probability \(\theta\); the low one has success probability \(1/4+\theta/2\), conditional on the entire past. Issuing a positive decision starts actual finishing work. The response program sees the decision and uses its retained state to select a proposal before the finalizer makes its fresh draw.

An installation check verifies which guard revision is in use. The good revision permits a surcharge \(\beta\) on the finalizer's conditional violation probability with \(0\le\beta\le1/32\); the bad revision has \(\beta=1/2\). The check authenticates this connection to the real mechanism. It does not reveal \(\theta\). The event \(I\) is both positive issuance and actual activation. Here the controller sees the bits, its genuine route receipt and any purchased probe. It never sees the installed parameter or the scorer's private outcome record when choosing its verdict. A separate scorer checks actual activation, violation and work on the installed route. Every world earns \(1/2\) for completed intake. Only a truly positive world with safe successful finishing after issuance earns the additional \(1/2\). Both verdicts are binary: stopping with a negative verdict is an error in a truly positive world, and positive issuance is an error in a truly negative world. The parameter \(\theta\) is the risk of the \emph{ordinary reference} operation. The cap below conditions instead on issuance by the \emph{delegated} policy, including its mistaken positive decisions. Appendix~\ref{app:unified} gives the source and carried-state dynamics.

\begin{theorem}[A common governed policy with exact cost, U.G3]\label{body:unified}
On the verified good branch of this base interface, require uniform binary-decision error at most \(1/3\), selective violation risk at most \(3/8\), positive issuance at least \(2/3\) on positive installations, and expected utility of at least \(1/2\) everywhere and \(25/32\) on positives. The minimum worst expected per-attempt acquisition is \(7/12\) and the minimum hard acquisition cap is one. The best high-or-skip policy costs \(2/3\) in expectation. A good guard report costs \(1/4\) once per installation and is a least realized sufficient setup in the stated catalog. These optima require the complete specified base interface, the matched pre-probe laws, and true issuance, work and violation scoring on the linked physical continuation. One policy gives selective risk at most \(5/16\) everywhere and \(5/32\) on positives, issues with probability at least \(1/4\) everywhere, and has expected utility at least \(25/32\) on positives.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[The price is forced by two indistinguishable starts]
The initial bit sequence uses a fixed hidden bit \(m\) and a carried bit \(v\) starting at zero. At each step it repeats \(v\) with probability \(p_m(\theta)=1/2+(-1)^m\theta(1-\theta)/2\), otherwise flips it, and retains the resulting bit. The two endpoints below have the same \(\theta(1-\theta)\). With all other installed settings and disclosed fields matched, the complete pre-probe record law agrees at \(\theta=1/4\) and \(\theta=3/4\). Let \(l,h\) be the probabilities of buying low or high on this pair. They are identical on the two worlds and \(l+h\le1\). The largest difference in positive declaration probabilities is \(l/4+h/2\), the weighted differences between the two bit laws. Errors at most \(1/3\) require a difference of at least \(1/3\). Hence
\[
 (3/8)l+h=(5/8)(l+2h)-(l+h)/4\ \ge\ 7/12 .
\]
With high or skip alone, \(h\ge2/3\). Buying high with probability \(2/3\), issuing on zero, and guessing fairly on skip attains this price and the same issuance law as the mixed-probe policy below. If the hard cap is below one then \(h=0\) and the required separation is impossible. To attain the bound, buy low with probability \(2/3\) and high with probability \(1/3\), and issue when the observed bit is zero. The issue probability is \(5/6-2\theta/3\), giving the claimed errors and exposure. The optimal quality lottery can be drawn before either the prelude or the token. It gains no extra price advantage from conditioning that choice on previous bits in this construction. The genuine route receipt is still needed to call the selected probe, and the responder's retained bit still changes physical finishing risk. On issuance the finalizer's violation probability is \(\theta/4+a/16+\beta\), where the carried proposal \(a\) is the exclusive-or of \(m\) and the last bit \(v\), chosen before the fresh finalizer draw. Applying this bound at every reached finishing decision gives the selective bounds. On a positive world the expected utility is at least \(1/2+(1/2)(2/3)(1-5/32)=25/32\). A cheaper \(1/8\) ordinary-route inspection leaves a possible bad revision on a true-positive installation. Positive issuance is required there, and every issued cut has actual violation probability above \(1/2\). The \(1/4\) good-guard report excludes this obstruction; comprehensive inspection costs one.
\end{proof}

The nominal finalizer uses zero surcharge. Coupling it to the actual one with the same uniform word, the same carried proposal, and the same preceding record gives an exact increase \(\beta\Pr(I)\) in violation mass. Applicable numerical error does not relax a hard permission. A richer interface can beat the price, and a missing guard can invalidate the selective certificate. \paragraph{Why the prelude compresses exactly.}
At depth \(t\), let \(s\) be the number of times the bit stayed. The number of flips is \(t-s\), whose parity gives the last bit because the start was zero. The likelihood of any particular word with these counts is
\[
               p_m(\theta)^s[1-p_m(\theta)]^{t-s}.
\]
For example the three-step words \(001\) and \(011\), starting from zero, each have one flip, end at one and have the same likelihood in every fixed world. They deliver the same carried proposal and the same later legal options when the route agrees. All \(2^t\) words have positive support, yet exactly \(t+1\) stay counts suffice per declared route at depth \(t\). The likelihood above is per word; summing incoming flows retains the multiplicity of the words with the same count. Across depths the prelude graph grows quadratically. The checked correspondence covers the actual true event, route and work as well as the receipts. The grid theorem uses separate discrepancy and smoothness assumptions; the exact \(7/12\) proof uses the matched pair and the attaining policy directly.
