\section{What can an evaluation-aware system conceal?}\label{sec:strategy}
Return to the release agent. It may recognize a rehearsal, see a positive verdict and change its proposal afterward. An installed \emph{response rule} specifies its choices at each information state it can reach. The same rule and its memory persist into production. A complete record includes everything the auditor can legitimately observe, including timing and receipts. A future private audit choice is unavailable to the responder. These conditions describe the strategic model and determine which comparisons are valid.

\paragraph{A single bit can separate each pair and still fail.}
Suppose one test is allowed. For a positive installation the bit is always zero or always one, depending on which positive installation was supplied. For a negative one the bit is fair. Each individual positive law differs from the negative law. Yet an equal mixture of the two positive laws produces exactly the negative distribution. An auditor whose error were below one half in every installation would also beat one half in that mixture comparison, which is impossible. Repeated draws from the same installation would give a different experiment.

\begin{theorem}[The best guaranteed binary verdict, A.G1]\label{body:strategy}
Let \(\mathcal D\) be the complete finite, nonempty class of permitted deterministic audit-and-stop plans on a finite interface. Either verdict is lawful at every stop. For each plan \(d\), let \(S_d\) be its possible stopped records. Let \(x_w(h)\) be the chance that world \(w\) supplies the data of record \(h\) under its prescribed commands. These coordinates together describe \emph{one world across all plans}, and sum to one on each plan's stopped records. For each nonempty label class take all mixtures of its world vectors and their limits, giving closed convex sets \(K_+\) and \(K_-\). With private randomization of the design and its terminal report, the least worst-world binary error \(R_*\) is attained and satisfies
\[
 R_*=\frac{1-\gamma}{2},\qquad
 \gamma=\min_{\substack{0\le\lambda\le1\\ p\in K_+,\,q\in K_-}}
         \max_{d\in\mathcal D}\sum_{h\in S_d}
            \big|\lambda p(h)-(1-\lambda)q(h)\big|.
\]
Here \(\lambda\) is an adversarial weight on the positive class. The sum measures the evidence available to a particular plan for distinguishing the two weighted transcript laws. The least favorable weight and laws must hold simultaneously across the design menu. Chance is optimal exactly when the two closed hulls intersect.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed weighted laws and a fixed design, choose the more probable label at each stopped record. The sum of the smaller masses, the resulting error, is half of one minus the displayed separation for that design. Finite-dimensional minimax exchanges the privately randomized auditor with the least favorable weighted laws. Zero separation forces equal total weights by normalization on any plan, then equality on every observable coordinate. Conversely a shared hull vector with equal weights makes every design guess. Theorem~\ref{adv:ag1} gives the closure and attainment details. The mixture is an adversarial comparison; no distribution of deployed installations is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Which verified facts remove a common-policy obstruction?, A.G2]\label{body:blockers}
Fix \(m\ge1\) authorized deterministic complete policies. Their private mixtures are points of the probability simplex \(\Delta_m\). First restrict this simplex to the closed convex set \(X_{\rm guard}\) obeying every original hard guard in every world to which it applies. If this set is empty, no fact selection works. Assume it is nonempty. For each world \(w\) let \(P_w\subseteq X_{\rm guard}\) retain the mixtures also meeting its affine requirements on genuine performance. Selecting facts can remove a performance alternative; it never enlarges \(X_{\rm guard}\). Both designated, realizable opposite-label reference worlds satisfy every candidate fact, and the positive reference has a positive service requirement. An exact, nondisturbing fact has a known satisfaction set and nonnegative additive price. A collection of realized facts permits one common policy on its survivors exactly when it excludes at least one member of \emph{every} group of at most \(m\) worlds whose feasible sets have empty intersection. The cheapest sufficient selection is a minimum weighted hitting set (Theorem~\ref{adv:ag2}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A surviving infeasible group rules out a common policy. Conversely, closed subsets of the compact fixed guard set with empty total intersection have an already infeasible finite subfamily. Its affine dimension is at most \(m-1\), so Helly's theorem gives a witness of at most \(m\) worlds. To remove a witness, at least one selected fact must be false in at least one of its worlds.
\end{proof}
A safeguard read can establish that a trusted finalizer was present throughout. This excludes worlds whose competing decoder or faulty route made useful issuance impossible. A functioning read cannot install a safeguard, establish its own accuracy, or exchange a hard permission for a small risk. Theorem~\ref{adv:ag3} gives a routed strategic service with priced diagnostics and actual finishing work. Theorems~\ref{thm:t21}--\ref{thm:t24} address ordinary transcript similarity, information acquired over time and validity under adaptive sampling.
