# T15 · Conditional laws of the reachable audit

For a finite authorized common protocol, expand its transcript as its initial observation and then alternating auditor decisions and recorded responses. A decision can be STOP. Every private auditor coin can be drawn before the relevant decision or kept in the recorded audit filtration. At equal histories the auditor's conditional choice kernel is common. Neither decision depends on the hidden model index. The response row under a selected query is the model's genuine conditional law given that history and query. There is no memory restart unless reset is one of the recorded authorized operations.

Induct on the length of the public prefix. The initial law agrees by hypothesis. Multiplication by the common action/stop kernel preserves the probability of the chosen decision. On a positive prefix with a sampled response, multiplication by the equal applicable response row preserves the extension probability. A null prefix has null extensions in both models. Padding with a common terminal marker handles any early stopping at or before the finite horizon. Thus complete transcripts have equal laws, including every stopped branch. Any common randomized map of the transcript to a claim preserves equality.

For the converse, for a history h and allowed query q choose the permitted protocol in which (h,q) has positive probability. Equality of its complete laws gives the same positive marginal denominator for (h,q), and the same numerator for each possible following response, by prefix projection. Taking their ratio gives the equal response rows. Row equality is proved only where the history and query are jointly exercisable. If a legality difference is itself observable, it is a response or initial observation and must be included. If the legal menu is known to the analyst but action changes are not admissible in both worlds, only the matched experiment is compared.

If two target-distinct models have equal laws for the actual procedure, no procedure with zero error on both can make two incompatible claims on a common probability law. T21 gives the quantitative statement. Repeating separate runs is included when every conditional row of the combined protocol agrees, for example under faithful independent resets. Equality of standalone margins alone does not establish that premise for carried state.
