# P1 · Control–certificate separation

D-35 p. 4 and Appendix A p. 12. **Constructor verdict:** the existential separation follows for one fixed history quotient given complete certificate fidelity. A complete typed witness resolves the assumption about other representatives in the split case.

Let the worlds have opposite target labels $0,1$. Use exactly four history representatives $h_0,h_1,w_0,w_1$, with $h_i,w_i$ compatible exactly with world $i$. Take a fixed certificate partition with blocks $\{h_0,h_1\},\{w_0\},\{w_1\}$. A zero-cost source setup from an open root reaches open checkpoints $u_i$ with $H(u_i)=h_i$ and the same visible setup outcome. The distinct controller observation at $u_i$ records $i$. Both unit-cost actions $q_0,q_1$ are legal. At $u_i$, $q_i$ leads to the witness $w_i$ and correct closure. The opposite action leads to an actionless open trap with representative $h_i$. The setup is the only route from the root. One constant authority operator licenses the declared actions when their ordinary preconditions hold. This defines all reachability, support, costs, and nonempty fibers for the budget-one claim.

Let $Z$ be a fixed quotient of the carrier used both as the controller's checkpoint signal and as the certificate equivalence. Certificate fidelity preserves the intended open checkpoints before the repair and the correct closed target class at each correctly repaired terminal. Other visible channels remain as declared.

- If $Z(h_0)=Z(h_1)$, the full post-setup observable histories are identical. The same selected repair action fails on one path. Randomizing also keeps a failing positive-support choice. Every legitimate budget-one policy under this observation choice fails to close surely.
- If $Z(h_0)\ne Z(h_1)$, certificate fidelity at the terminals requires that no $w_i$ share a $Z$ cell with any opposite-target history. In particular $w_{1-i}$ cannot share a cell with $h_i$. The other opposite-target history $h_{1-i}$ already has a different quotient. Those two histories exhaust the opposite-target compatible possibilities. Thus each $h_i$ cell is prematurely target-homogeneous, violating fidelity at $u_i$.

The original separate $P$ and $\omega$ permit the controller to choose $q_i$ while leaving each pre-repair certificate open. The proof specifies the complete carrier and rules out cross-merges by checking the terminal labels as well as premature closure. Splitting two particular representatives in an arbitrary larger carrier by itself would fail to guarantee that their fibers are homogeneous. A checkpoint-dependent pair of different certificate and decision representations is outside the one-fixed-quotient claim. Computational falsification enumerates all partitions of this witness.
