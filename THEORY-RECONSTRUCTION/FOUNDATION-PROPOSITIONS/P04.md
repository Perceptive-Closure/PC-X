# P4 · Zero-gap criterion

D-35 p. 7 and Appendix G p. 14. **Constructor verdict:** the equivalence follows by policy inclusion. Finite source atoms and nonnegative costs give the attained finite optima on this model whenever proper finite continuation is possible.

Every R-frozen proper policy is a proper unrestricted policy of the same cost. Hence frozen value is at least unrestricted value. A baseline minimizer using no R-touching action on every supported branch remains feasible under the R freeze. Its cost attains the common lower bound and the gap is zero. If the gap is zero and both values are finite and attained, a frozen minimizer is an unrestricted minimizer and avoids R touch on all supported branches.

For finite action costs from one finite set, choose any finite proper tree with worst cost $B$. Let $\delta$ be the smallest positive action cost if such a cost exists. The possible sums at most $B$ have each positively priced action count bounded by $\lfloor B/\delta\rfloor$. The zero-cost counts contribute zero. The set of such real sums is finite, and each tree's worst cost is a sum of one of its finite branches. Minimizing over the nonempty subset of attainable sums below the witnessed bound gives attainment. A zero-cost cycle without a proper exit never qualifies as a finite closer. If no proper unrestricted closer exists the R penalty is undefined. The structural case of an infinite R-frozen value with finite baseline does not give a zero gap.
