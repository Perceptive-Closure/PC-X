## D-35 contract interpretation

The admitted cell is [H(s)]_{P_R(s)} over the common universe U_H. CR(s) is the existential image of that whole cell under world–history compatibility. The named value H(s) need never be itself compatible with any world. For a reachable checkpoint the existential image is nonempty. The sealed target is constant on that set exactly at closure. Controller history consists of the initial observation and the interleaving of chosen actions, visible outcome labels and following observations. Beliefs include each physical checkpoint consistent with that history. The next action must be authorized, source-enabled and survive the freeze at every member. Source-atomic touch uses the union over all supported successor edges. No effect is split. These premises describe a closed transition-support model. A latent constraint coupling otherwise composable edges across time belongs in the physical state if it constrains true support.

A stopping leaf requires closure at every checkpoint it represents. A demand that the controller also report which closed target value holds requires one further condition, namely constancy of the terminal target label over the leaf. D-35's first-order closure is checkpoint-relative. Adding the reporting condition leaves the following transport proof valid when the relation preserves terminal labels.

## T03 support transport

Fix a freeze, and translate both graphs to the same observed-history labels by the common observation-indexed action correspondence and the common (observation, action)-indexed outcome correspondence. A successor label used below is the pair of visible outcome and next observation. These maps give a consistent translation of controller histories, including histories whose underlying checkpoints are not unique. Let R be the stated bisimulation after translation. Given belief B on the left and C on the right, assume two-way total coverage by R. Every b in B has a related c in C and every c in C has a related b in B.

For any action allowed at every member of B, enabled-set and touch preservation under each related pair makes it legal at every member of C, and conversely. If both use this action, then the successor belief for any given observed label is the union of all corresponding successors of all admissible hidden checkpoints. For each such successor on the left, choose a related predecessor on the right and use forth matching under the same label. This supplies a related successor in the right belief. Use back matching for the converse. Therefore the corresponding successor beliefs retain two-way coverage, even when multiple physical successors share a label. The same labels reach nonempty beliefs on the two sides. Coverage and status preservation give equality of the stop test. The stronger terminal-reporting test follows by terminal-label preservation.

Recursively copy the same policy node on each **observable** history. Induction uses the entire belief reached by that history. It never picks a different continuation for two hidden successors represented at one node. The resulting set of observable action-history paths is preserved in both directions. Along every matched history the action costs are equal. Thus any proper finite tree and its transport have identical worst-branch cost. Taking infima over both directions proves all eight values, with the correct empty-feasibility infinity convention. No attainment is needed for this argument. Point identification additionally requires a nonempty completion set and such equivalence at all realized entry locators.

D-35 Appendix D invokes checkpoint-level induction and says each successor pair lies in the relation. Forth/back supplies selected matches and does not assert an arbitrary cross-product. The whole-belief argument supplies the missing uniform-continuation step under the observation-consistent interface stated in the theorem. The policy witness is source-interface-relative. Allowing separate renamings at aliased hidden checkpoints invalidates the conclusion, as shown by the action and outcome witnesses below.

## Proper finite trees and costs

For an arbitrary finite contract form the graph over nonempty history beliefs. It has at most 2^|S|−1 vertices. If an optimal or candidate proper finite tree repeats a belief along a branch, graft the later subtree at the earlier copy. Its legality and terminal checks depend only on the same belief. The remaining supported suffix histories are the same, so the replacement cannot cost more than the original subtree, whose paths through the later occurrence first accrue nonnegative costs. The graft strictly decreases the finite node count. Repeated pruning produces a tree with no repeated belief on any path and depth at most the number of nonempty beliefs. There are finitely many such observation trees, so any finite infimum is attained. Equivalently one can compute the finite-depth recurrence from stopped beliefs at value zero and all others at infinity. A zero-cost open self-loop may satisfy a falsely favorable Bellman equation with no proper policy. The rank or finite-depth witness blocks that inference.

An observation refinement with a known projection to the coarse visible history, and the same physical actions, support, closure and costs, weakly lowers the value. A coarse policy composed with the projection remains legal branch by branch. This monotonicity is about controller access and holds separately from representation refinement. A refined certificate partition can change closure, and semantic changes to authority or support invalidate the fixed-contract comparison.
