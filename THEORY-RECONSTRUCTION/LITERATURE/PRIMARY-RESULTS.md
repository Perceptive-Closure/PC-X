# Primary-source comparison inventory

The sources and their overlapping claims were consulted for the present bounded claims. Additional first-hand checks and citations are in the individual workstream records. This is a bounded literature review and the originality of individual structural specializations remains a separate matter.

| Topic | Primary source and result read | Consequence for PC-X |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy verification and change impact | Fisler et al. construct decision diagrams from supplied XACML policies and compare their semantics. [Margrave](https://cs.brown.edu/people/sk/Publications/Papers/Published/fkmt-verif-change-impact-xacml/) | T02 asks whether the source warrants the resource counterfactual to be evaluated in the first place. Policy difference machinery still addresses an important downstream question. |
| Partial identification | Horowitz and Manski bound the effect of unobserved outcomes and regressors under nonresponse. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(97)00077-8) | T11/T19/T20/T25 use feasible images, identified ranges and coverage bounds. Operational missingness and sharp joint constraints must be stated. |
| Partial reward identifiability | Skalse et al. characterize invariances of reward-data observation objects and downstream tasks. [ICML paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/skalse23a.html) | This is a close precedent for the distinction between target and model identification. PC uses authenticated action resources, policy feasibility and admissible measurements. |
| Discernibility and test cover | Skowron and Rauszer form discernibility conditions and reducts from information-system rows. [Chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-015-7975-9_21) | T04–T06 are deterministic target-relative discernibility and weighted cover with explicit query legitimacy. |
| Active learning | Golovin, Krause and Ray use cross-class edge cutting in Bayesian acquisition. [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2010/hash/1e6e0a04d20f50967c64dac2d639a577-Abstract.html) | Equal-answer target disputes are established objects. PC's exact minimax and realized models require their own cost assumptions. |
| Decision regions | Javdani et al. allow overlapping decisions whose regions cover the compatible hypotheses, and prove a Bayesian competition result. [AISTATS paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v33/javdani14.html) | One successful governed policy defines a region. T29.b's upper-order readiness obstruction and optimization use this structure with a finite operational oracle and fixed-price minimax. |
| Decision trees and complexity | Hyafil and Rivest prove hardness for optimal binary expected decision-tree path cost. Karp gives foundational finite reductions. [Tree hardness](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/HR76.pdf) · [Karp](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9) | These do not subsume the exact PC embedding or the binary paired feasibility problem by themselves. The underlying reduction and pseudo-polynomial boundary are familiar. |
| Implicit optimization and symbolic verification | Chandrasekaran et al. treat an implicit family with a separation oracle. Dechter eliminates factors with width dependence. [Implicit hitting set](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611973082.48) · [Bucket elimination](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(99)00059-4) | T14.a is a known implicit optimization pattern. Local crossing and paired capped factor elimination instantiate it for governed finite scores. No general new complexity theory results. |
| Blocker and support duality | Edmonds and Fulkerson formulate bottleneck duality via clutters. [Bottleneck extrema](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9800(70)80083-7) | T12's useful modeling distinction is a support set for an entire legal observation policy and a residual budget, not just one path or a new blocker principle. |
| Exact causal abstraction | Rubenstein et al. give interventional exact transformations. Beckers and Halpern refine the abstraction types. [Causal consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00819) · [Abstracting causal models](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/halpern/papers/abstraction.pdf) | Commuting transformations with observational meaning are prior. T18 checks source rows, authorized control and actual event scoring under named abstractions. |
| Causal transportability | Pearl and Bareinboim study transfer given structural and selection information. [External validity](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-STS486) | T18 offers a finite certificate and conditional law bounds, not a complete decision procedure for structural causal transport. |
| Approximate stochastic simulation | Bian and Abate establish finite trace-TV bounds from approximate bisimulation. Haesaert et al. refine policies on stochastic abstraction interfaces. [Bisimulation and traces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04547) · [Policy refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07622) | Coupling, product survival and policy-under-projection are prior. T18's event-sensitive common lower law comes from independently constrained conditional factors. T26 checks actual effect preservation. |
| Controlled and sequential sensing | Nitinawarat, Atia and Veeravalli derive error-exponent results for experiment choice. Kaufmann, Cappé and Garivier give a stopped change-of-measure inequality. [Controlled sensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0858) · [Fixed-confidence change of measure](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v17/kaufman16a.html) | T21/T22 use established lower bounds. Expected allocation and optimal worst-cost or fixed-budget coverage are distinct. |
| Time-valid confidence and robust inference | Howard et al. derive nonasymptotic time-uniform confidence sequences from supermartingale tools. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240) | T23's likelihood set and T24's optional sampling reasoning use known mechanisms. Finite admissible score intervals, independent fidelity and common-response classes determine the PC-specific application. |
| Imperfect information and shielding | Chatterjee et al. use knowledge states to solve imperfect-information games. Alshiekh et al. construct safety shields. [Observation-based games](https://lmcs.episciences.org/1094) · [Formal shielding](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/11797/11797-13-15325-1-2-20201228.pdf) | T28/T29 derive both lawful supported safety and useful completion under semantic uncertainty. Belief-state synthesis and safety enforcement are established. |
| Compositional verification | Kwiatkowska et al. give assume-guarantee rules for probabilistic systems. [Primary paper](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/39607/1/tacas10.pdf) | The finite T27 bound uses independently checked local retention and global containment of events. It is a sharp result for the chosen event information, without substituting for an automaton assume-guarantee calculus. |
| Evaluation awareness | Greenblatt et al. report behavior dependent on a model's perceived evaluation/training context in a specified setup. [Alignment faking study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093) | It motivates considering conditional response and selection effects. The scripted PC-X X09 evidence does not establish behavior or confidence for unrestricted models. |
| Scalable oversight | Irving, Christiano and Amodei propose a debate game as an oversight mechanism. [AI safety via debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899) | T30.S concerns structural identification and bounded readback complexity, a different objective. An unqualified claim about general scalable oversight is left open. |
