# Complexity of the generalized strategic packet

## Representation

Let \(n\) be the number of exactly represented worlds, \(A_h\) the common allowed acquisition and report commands at record \(h\), \(Q=\sum_h |A_h|\), \(N=|S|\) the number of pre-report stop records and \(L=|J|\) the number of verified facts. Count all stored rational coefficients by their numerator and denominator bit lengths. A controller tree can have size exponential in the numeric horizon. Expanding a source program or proving legal reachability, exact membership and physical correspondence is separate from running an optimizer.

The G1 finite realization formulation has \(Q+1\) variables, one balance equality per choice record, \(n\) worst-error inequalities with at most \(N\) report coefficients each, and nonnegativity conditions. These include all contingent acquisition and legal reporting rules. Bit-polynomial rational LP time applies in these sizes. Complete histories and legal phase/authority states must be represented, and cost state may be needed before enforcing a supported cap. Arbitrary additional nonlocal restrictions on pure designs can instead be included via their explicitly listed tests, of which there may be exponentially many even in the size of the tree. The compactness and finite atom bounds for arbitrary signed images are statements of existence. Without a computable representation for membership and separating constraints they are not continuum search algorithms.

A G2 selection can be evaluated via a rational LP in the fixed \(m\)-simplex and all remaining worldwise constraints. One can enumerate the subsets of \(L\) facts, or progressively solve a finite weighted hitting-set master with infeasibility cuts. In the latter algorithm, at most \(n\) additional LPs per infeasible candidate shrink its retained-world family to one inclusion-minimal blocker. This core has size at most \(m\). There are at most \(\sum_{i=1}^{\min(n,m)}\binom{n}{i}\) candidate blocker world sets and at most \(2^L\) possible distinct fact cuts. The set-cover reduction proves NP-hardness even with four deterministic verdict tables on the two possible bit receipts and smallest blockers of size two. No general efficiency claim is made for the price optimizer, independent receipt acquisition, or structure of the original model.

The G3 fixture has a fixed public decision tree. The exact lower certificates use two worlds and 144 deterministic policies in the accompanying exhaustive check. The paper proof obtains the bound from one inequality on conditional purchase probabilities. Its upper guarantee is analytic uniformly in the continuous parameter and legal programs. The rational checker uses 128 finite dry/continuation table choices with degree-two Bernstein certificates on the entire parameter interval. These costs concern computation of the mathematical fixture, separate from the acquisition tariff, real work, and physical policy verification.
