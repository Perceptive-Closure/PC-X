## T30.A.G1 · The simultaneous game

Let \(N=|S|\). Let \(\mathcal T\) be the finite set of complete deterministic binary tests. A test \(i=(d,t)\) uses acquisition table \(d\) and terminal map \(t:S_d\to\{+,-\}\). Every such \(t\) is legal for the identification problem. Given a privately randomized algorithm, presample its private randomness and condition on the resulting table and report rule. Those determine its behavior on each record on which it must act. Its risk is the mixture of the risks of the resulting tests in \(\mathcal T\). The completion is fixed before these draws. Even conditional on a table, each compatible history has its specified \(x_\omega(h)\) because the only information about the controller disclosed to the responder is that in the executed prefix and declared signals. Formally, the fixed source program and the responder's conditioned hidden state evolve under each successive executed command, so the probability of the intervened prefix does not depend on unused commands at other records. This applies to a public choice of protocol announced by an actual prefix command, whose declaration is part of the prefix. Every mixture of legal tests can also be implemented by a private initial draw. Reports and their actual continuations are included separately when optimizing a physical objective.

For all \(p_b\in K_b\) and every \(d\), the vector \(p_b|_{S_d}\) has nonnegative entries and coordinates summing to one. These conditions hold on actual vectors and are preserved by the convex hull and closure. Write the mathematical opponent as a pair of weighted vectors,
\[
U=\{(u_+,u_-): u_+=\lambda p_+,\quad
u_-=(1-\lambda)p_-,\quad
0\le \lambda\le1,\ p_b\in K_b\}.
\]
This is compact as a continuous image of a compact product. It is convex because a convex combination of weighted pairs is again a weighted pair after dividing each sign's combined mass by its total weight if positive. For zero weight an arbitrary vector of that sign suffices. Here \(U\) is exactly the closed convex hull of \((x_\omega,0)\) for positives and \((0,x_\omega)\) for negatives. Indeed finite convex combinations have the indicated pair form with finite mixtures from the respective classes. Approximating each \(p_b\in K_b\) by such finite mixtures proves the reverse inclusion after closure.

A pure test's risk against this opponent is
\[
   L_{d,t}(u)=
       \sum_{h\in S_d:t(h)=-}u_+(h)
       +\sum_{h\in S_d:t(h)=+}u_-(h).
\]
For every controller mixture the supremum of this linear loss over actual worlds equals the maximum over \(U\), by convexification and continuity. For clarity, a finite-dimensional separation proof yields the required minimax interchange. Map \(U\) into the compact convex set \(Q=\{(L_i(u))_{i\in\mathcal T}:u\in U\}\). Set \(r=\max_{q\in Q}\min_{i\in\mathcal T}q_i\). The minimum of finitely many coordinates is continuous, so some \(q_*\) attains \(r\). Since every coordinate of \(q_*\) is at least \(r\), any policy mixture has worst loss at least \(r\). The open convex orthant \(\{v:v_i>r\ \forall i\}\) is disjoint from \(Q\). Separating these convex sets gives a nonzero \(p\) such that \(\sup_{q\in Q}p\cdot q\le\inf_{v_i>r}p\cdot v\). A negative component would make the latter infimum unbounded below. Thus \(p\) is nonnegative and can be normalized to sum to one, whereupon the right side is \(r\). The corresponding private mixture attains \(r\). This supplies equality and attainment even if some world's supremum is not attained.

For fixed \(u\) and \(d\), the report minimizes loss by taking its smaller contribution independently at each stopped record. Its Bayes loss is
\[
   \sum_{h\in S_d}\min(u_+(h),u_-(h))
    =\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sum_{h\in S_d}|u_+(h)-u_-(h)|\right).
\]
Minimizing also over \(d\), then maximizing over \(u\in U\), gives the sealed formula. The fair private constant guess is allowed, so the value is in \([0,1/2]\). A zero optimizing gap gives \(u_+=u_-\) at all \(h\) since every \(h\in S\) belongs to some \(S_d\). Summing over any nonempty table's stopped records gives \(\lambda=1-\lambda=1/2\), yielding \(p_+=p_-\). Conversely, a common vector with \(\lambda=1/2\) gives zero gap. Equality on each \(S_d\) implies equality for any randomized test, even jointly with its independent seed and its report. The two cohorts draw a completion before the seed and keep it fixed thereafter. The construction never chooses replacement completions at later histories.

For each sign, affine dependence permits any finite convex combination of points in \(\mathbb R^N\) to be reduced to at most \(N+1\) constituents. If there are more, take nonzero coefficients of an affine dependence summing to zero and subtract the largest multiple that maintains all weights nonnegative. Some weight vanishes and the vector and total mass are unchanged. With a compact class image this bound writes its convex hull as the image of a compact simplex times \(N+1\) copies of the class image. The convex hull is then already closed, and any maximizing least-favorable pair uses at most the stated number of actual completions per sign. If compactness fails, approximate each \(p_b\) in an optimizing weighted pair by such actual convex combinations. The minimum Bayes loss over the finite pure tests is continuous in \(u\). A sufficiently accurate approximation still has minimum Bayesian error greater than any fixed \(\delta<R_*\). The same two approximating mixtures and weight therefore refute \(\delta\) for every deterministic or randomized design. A compact parameter space gives compact images only when the relevant sign images are closed and the laws are continuous on those pieces, or when compactness is independently proved.

### Finite rational synthesis

With the indicated full common menu, augment the public tree by the binary report actions. For every allowable choice \(a\) at a controller record \(h\), use the nonnegative counterfactual private realization weight \(z_{h,a}\). At each record, the sum of outgoing weights is the weight of the incoming controller choice on that history, or one for an initial record without such a choice. At a genuine observed branch, each successor uses that same incoming weight. Its actual chance probability is already in the prefix law of that successor. No probability is divided by the chance of an observation. Convert a feasible realization solution to choice probabilities by taking ratios to its incoming weight when positive, and use any authorized continuation when zero. Then by induction its own prefix-choice product is the stated weight. Conversely a behavioral controller gives such weights, and a mixed policy satisfies these identities after averaging its deterministic realization weights. Thus root-to-report realization vectors are exactly attainable for this menu.

Minimize \(r\) subject to the realization constraints and, for each explicitly listed \(w\),
\[
  \sum_{h\in S} x_w(h)\,z_{h,\mathrm{wrong}(Y(w))}\le r,
\]
using only the report-choice coordinates present at each such \(h\). The product of the mediated prefix probability with that weight is the true probability of traversing that record and choosing that report, including the prior commands. This gives a finite rational LP polynomial in the indicated expanded input. Arbitrary global restrictions on design tables can be handled by an explicit finite mixture LP if the admissible table-and-report list is available. The local realization LP does not presume that those restrictions are local. For an actual implementation, rows and full-interface exhaustion are data obligations rather than consequences of the LP.

### One-read controls

For the first profiles in the seal, assign weight \(1/2\) to each of the two worlds of its sign. Both mixtures give probability \(1/2\) to bit 1 under both probes, and an immediate stop carries no data. Every fixed opposite pair differs on both probe laws, while the simultaneous mixture proves \(R_*=1/2\).

For the second profiles, immediate stop is uninformative. Each individual probe matches the positive world to one of the two negative worlds. Assign positive prior \(3/5\) and negative prior \(2/5\), the latter uniform across the two negative worlds. In either probe the weighted masses for bit 1 are \(3/10\) versus \(3/10\), and for bit 0 are \(3/10\) versus \(1/10\). The best Bayes risk from either probe is \(3/10+1/10=2/5\), and immediate stop gives \(2/5\). For an upper bound choose either probe with equal probability, report \(+\) after 0, and report \(-\) with probability \(4/5\) after 1. The positive world gives a 1 with probability \(1/2\), whereas each negative gives a 1 with probability \(3/4\) under the randomized selection. The two signed error rates are \((4/5)(1/2)=2/5\) and \(1-(4/5)(3/4)=2/5\).

## T30.A.G2 · Source-fact selection

After admitting \(L\subseteq J\), let \(W_L=\bigcap_{j\in L}F_j\), with \(W_\varnothing=W\). A common policy is precisely an element of \(\bigcap_{\omega\in W_L}P_\omega\), under the fixed-menu and preserved-row assumptions. If a blocker is wholly retained, this set is empty. Conversely, if the intersection is empty, the complements of the \(P_\omega\) cover the compact simplex, giving a finite subcover. The corresponding finite collection of good sets has empty intersection. The affine hull of the simplex has dimension \(m-1\), so finite Helly's theorem gives a subcollection of at most \(m\) sets with empty intersection. Thus some blocker is wholly retained. A blocker is retained exactly if \(L\cap K(B)=\varnothing\). Minimizing \(\sum_{j\in L}c_j\) subject to all cuts yields the stated exact criterion. The two designated worlds survive every selection. If a feasible policy is implemented, it satisfies the positive useful-service floor on the designated positive world. This result requires closed constraints. An advertised strict positive denominator can use an explicit weak bound by a fixed positive constant.

For finite rational inputs, check any stated blocker by LP on the corresponding sets. One exact algorithm tests at most \(2^{|J|}\) selections for minimum cost. Another starts with no cuts, solves the finite master hitting-set problem, checks a proposed minimizing selection's joint feasible policy LP, and adds a cut if infeasible. A blocker can be found by removing retained-world constraints one world at a time whenever infeasibility persists. The final inclusion-minimal core has size at most \(m\) by finite Helly. Its cut either is empty, proving that no fact selection suffices, or excludes the current selection and permits another iteration. If feasible, the selection is globally optimal because all generated cuts are necessary. Commands, conditional laws and genuine effects remain those of the actual installed source in every surviving world. A library fact can concern a physical condition checked by a different instrument or source proof. Actuating a new mechanism and admitting a fact about the existing mechanism have separate semantics.

### Hardness even with pair blockers

Reduce ordinary finite Set Cover with universe \(U_0\), subsets \(S_j\subseteq U_0\), and unit costs. Use two designated realizable worlds \(w_+,w_-\), and an extra \(w_u\) for each \(u\in U_0\). At a mandatory safe free read the actual bit is 1 in \(w_+\) and every \(w_u\), and 0 in \(w_-\). A lawful \(+\) invokes one live finish; a lawful \(-\) stops. At live finish \(w_+\) has no defect and supplies one useful item. \(w_-\) has a defect from an independently specified unhealthy load, while each \(w_u\) has a defect from an actual relay write on its extra enabled maintenance route \(u\). Hard reserves include that possible write. The direct reference always finishes. Its risk cutoff \(1/2\) defines the signs \(+,-,-\) for the respective kinds of world. Require perfect binary decision, zero whole-attempt risk and positive service \(1\) in \(w_+\). Both threshold and all rows are rational. Each world alone is feasible. A retained \(w_u\) and \(w_+\) cannot be jointly identified because their only lawful receipt has the same law. Every collection without \(w_+\) permits refusal, and every collection without the rogue worlds permits a verdict by the read bit. Hence the inclusion-minimal blockers are exactly the indicated pairs.

Fact \(j\) independently checks that the installed relay has no enabled write among the routes \(S_j\). This holds in both \(w_+,w_-\) and excludes exactly the \(w_u\) with \(u\in S_j\). The actual installation in \(w_+\) realizes the given all-true fact vector. Each check is a primitive condition on relay authority and does not determine the label of the clean negative. Feasible fact selections are exactly the set covers with the same cost. The source tables, facts, and complete deterministic binary policies have a polynomial-size explicit encoding. This proves NP-hardness with the claimed non-vacuity and pair blockers.

## T30.A.G3 · Matched heterogeneous optimum

### Reference and carried source

For \(b=+\), the live left and right defect ranges are respectively \([1/8,1/4]\) and \([1/4,3/8]\). For \(b=-\) they are \([5/8,3/4]\) and \([3/4,7/8]\). These are valid conditional on each concrete cut, including the actual verdict, full carried state and chosen proposal. The direct reference has a fair intake and a single live finish almost surely. Averaging its independently checked reference-cue rows puts its risk in \([3/16,5/16]\) for \(+\) and in \([11/16,13/16]\) for \(-\), giving \(Y=b\) uniformly. The linked audit reaches the live row only on issuance. The direct and linked row intervals hold under both cues without requiring the distribution of the proposal to be the same. For example a responder can store a private pre-intake bit and update it on receipt of the route token, then choose that stored bit on the reference cue and its Boolean complement on \(+\). At the same \(b,s,k\), varying the proposal between 0 and 1 changes the actual defect probability by \(1/16\). A responder that also uses its private bit to choose the earlier \(j\) can correlate the live proposal with the bought record. The bound is still pointwise on the actual carried cut. The proof uses genuine event correspondence and almost-sure completion after issuance, as declared, and counts the pre-report stages separately as safe.

### Lower bound on every eligible policy

Use the two admitted worlds at \(s=0\) with \(j=0\) throughout the audit, one per nominal sign. Use an equal mathematical prior on this pair. Denote its averages by an overbar. Before the read, the token and the controller's own private randomness and skip/buy decision reveal no target information in this pair. Let \(\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]\) be the conditional purchase probabilities on \(L,R\) respectively. They agree across the pair. Given the read and route, the best posterior correctness is \(3/4\) on \(L\) and \(7/8\) on \(R\), for either read bit. This holds even conditional on the controller's random seed, since actual source draws are fresh with the exact rows. With no read, the posterior correctness of either issued label is \(1/2\).

The gain \(\overline{\Pr(D)}/2-\overline{\Pr(E)}\) sums conditional contributions over the actual operative data. At any stopped record, abstention contributes zero. An announced label contributes \(1/2\) less its posterior error, so the maximum gain at a read on either route is its correctness minus \(1/2\). At no read the maximum is zero. Hence the joint coverage and wrong-label constraints imply
\[
   \frac{7}{32}=\frac{3}{8}-\frac{5}{32}
   \le \frac12\overline{\Pr(D)}-\overline{\Pr(E)}
   \le \frac{\alpha}{8}+\frac{3\beta}{16}.
\]
Equivalently \(4\alpha+6\beta\ge7\). In particular \(\beta\ge1/2\), so some supported path has the price-3 read. For each of the matched worlds the expected acquisition price obeys
\[
    \frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{3\beta}{2}
      \ge \frac74-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ge\frac54.
\]
This lower bound uses the entire legal menu and arbitrary randomized stopping or verdicts. It continues to hold when the additional physical constraints are imposed, and does not equate expected and supported costs.

If the identity is chosen independently of the genuine token, with probabilities \(p\) for \(A\) and \(q\) for \(B\), then \(\alpha\le p,\ \beta\le q\) and \(p+q\le1\), even with branchwise skip decisions. The gain is at most \(3/16<7/32\). This rules out the matched precommitted subclass.

### Attaining policy and physical metrics

Always buy \(A\) on \(L\). Buy \(B\) with an independent fair coin on \(R\). Report the class indicated by a purchased bit and abstain when no bit is purchased. This yields \(\alpha=1,\ \beta=1/2\), a worst expected price \(5/4\), a worst supported price 3, and exactly \(3/4\) report coverage in every completion.

At each actual measurement cut, each class's bit correctness is at least \(3/4\) on \(L\) and \(7/8\) on \(R\), even under the admissible strategic \(j\). Thus unconditional wrong-label mass is at most \((1/2)(1/4)+(1/4)(1/8)=5/32\) in every completion. Positive issuance probability is at least \(3/4-5/32=19/32\) on \(+\) worlds. Their conditional live risk is at most \(3/8\) on each carried cut. Because the live finish is reached on every issuance, this gives the affine selective bound and useful service at least \((1-3/8)(19/32)=95/256\). More tightly for the whole attempt, the \(+\) risk is at most \((1/2)(1/4)+(1/4)(3/8)=7/32\). For \(-\) worlds, issuance is wrong and has probability at most \(5/32\). Their whole-attempt risk is therefore at most \((5/32)(7/8)=35/256\). These whole-attempt bounds are both below \(1/4\).

The two \(s=0,\ j=0\) matched worlds give the same support for every eligible pre-report policy. For any fixed private seed the chosen prefixes either skip or buy, and both receipt bits have positive chance on both worlds when a probe is bought. The seed distribution is the same, so every issuance event of positive probability on that positive world also occurs with positive probability on the paired negative world. There each carried live risk is at least \(5/8\) and a finish occurs on every issuance. Therefore the uniform selective cap \(1/2\) on both signs is incompatible with positive issuance on all positive completions.

### The finite safeguard instance

At \(s=0\) there are finitely many relevant deterministic responder tables through the cues, bit and proposals in the statement. Restrict the finite instance to the corresponding finite operative worlds, or take the finite tables as the extreme private-randomization choices when evaluating affine bounds. The deterministic tables include the matched \(j=0\) pair in the base class. Changing only the finalizer maintenance branch \(e\) leaves every pre-report law unchanged for such a program, by the stipulated sealed source rows. If an \(e\ne0\) is unexcluded, the nominal \(+\) realization with that branch has reference defect probability one and label \(-\). Compare it to the \(e=0\) version with label \(+\). For any policy their pre-report record and report laws are the same. Summing the two wrong-label probabilities gives their common coverage, which is at least \(3/4\) if both constraints hold. The two wrong-label caps sum to \(5/16<3/4\), so no feasible policy remains for that selection. For any remaining rogue branch these realizable pairs witness infeasibility with full legitimate transcripts.

Verifying a certificate excludes exactly the branches it names. One certificate always misses a branch. Any two cover all three and reduce the process to the base rows, where the stated policy attains the requirements. The cheapest pair costs \(2+3=5\). They preserve both designated signs, multiple responding programs and the possible \(1/16\) effect of each proposal. This selection optimum is conditional on the realized all-true certificate vector and the stipulated maintenance relay.
