## A.G1 · Simultaneous complete-law characterization

Every terminal deterministic binary rule can be put into a finite complete pure plan together with its acquisition table. An independently randomized acquisition table and arbitrary randomized terminal rule is, on the finite information tree, equivalent in outcome to a private lottery on these complete pure plans. At any stopped record reached by a chosen pure table the row weight is exactly \(x_\omega(h)\), regardless of unused choices in that table. The operative nonanticipation premise and fixed-world law are necessary for using one coordinate for every table with that executed record. Including public announcements and actual stop records prevents changes to available information or responder law from masquerading as pure record equivalence.

For each sign, \(K_b\) is compact, convex and nonempty in finite-dimensional space. For every \(d\), the vector entries in \(S_d\) remain nonnegative and sum to one on both \(K_b\) by linearity and closure. On pairs of weighted class laws set
\[
\mathcal C=\{(\lambda p_+,(1-\lambda)p_-):0\le\lambda\le1,\ p_b\in K_b\}.
\]
This compact image is convex. In a convex combination of two such pairs, the positive first-coordinate mass determines the new \(\lambda\). Normalize each class term by its weight and apply convexity of \(K_b\); a zero class weight requires no normalization. The supremum of a fixed randomized rule's error over actual completions equals its supremum expected error over \(\mathcal C\), because its score in each sign is continuous linear in the corresponding law. Thus it suffices to use bilinear minimax on the finite pure-plan simplex and \(\mathcal C\). The infimum is attained: the simplex is compact and the worst error is a supremum of continuous affine functions of the private plan weights.

For a fixed element of \(\mathcal C\) and deterministic table \(d\), the Bayes decision minimizes separately at each complete stopped record. The minimum error is
\[
\sum_{h\in S_d}\min\{\lambda p_+(h),(1-\lambda)p_-(h)\}
=\tfrac12\left(1-\sum_{h\in S_d}|\lambda p_+(h)-(1-\lambda)p_-(h)|\right).
\]
For this fixed prior some deterministic table attains the minimum over all tables, even though a robust minimax controller may mix. Minimax and reversal of the Bayes expression give the stated \(\gamma\). In particular \(\gamma=0\) forces \(\lambda=1/2\) by summing equality over the records of any one table. The zero terms for all tables then give \(p_+=p_-\) on their union \(S\). The converse uses the common vector at \(\lambda=1/2\). One matching law per separate deterministic design, potentially requiring different adverse mixtures, is insufficient.

If the sign images are compact, Carathéodory represents each point in their convex hull by at most \(|S|+1\) actual atoms and the hull is already closed. **Weights at a tie.** The phrase equal-weight opposing mixtures refers to total prior weight \(1/2\) on each sign. Individual atoms within each sign may need unequal weights. For a one-probe class of positive Bernoulli endpoints zero and one and a negative mean \(\sqrt2/2\), a common vector exists but cannot be obtained by a uniformly weighted finite mixture of positive atoms. In the noncompact-image case, approximate the optimizing \(p_b\) from the actual convex hulls, with each approximation itself using Carathéodory. Keep the same class weight \(\lambda\). Each table's weighted absolute-difference score changes continuously, and the maximum over the finite set also does. For \(\delta<R_*\), approximate closely enough that the Bayes error is still above \(\delta\) for the best table, hence also for every eligible audit. Exact matching can fail on a closed-hull boundary. For example a positive one-probe law of mean zero and negative laws of mean \(1/n\), \(n\ge2\), have intersecting closed hulls but every finite negative mixture has strictly positive mean.

In the local-menu case, maintain a nonnegative controller realization weight at each complete record, initialized as one at each actual initial information record. Split it among the outgoing possible common choices. Replicate the chosen action weight across actual delivered child records. Include terminal report choices explicitly. A fixed world contributes the product of the compiled intervened path likelihood and that realization weight at each stopped record and report. Its error is a linear combination of these contributions. Comparing every world error to a common epigraph gives an LP. A behavioral lift divides each action weight by the current record's weight on positive predecessors. Induction gives the same masses without assuming physical private-mixing frequencies for worlds. Zero-weight records need no operative choice; an arbitrary allowed prescription can complete the table where needed. The variable and row counts are polynomial in the explicit legal history tree and the supplied worlds. With rational rows and their explicit encodings, finite products and sums have polynomial bit lengths in that expanded input. The LP does not claim polynomial complexity in a succinct responder or arbitrary complete-design class. The minimax argument still uses an arbitrary explicit finite list of complete plans.

## A.G2 · Exact realized fact selection

On a selected fact set \(L\), the surviving class is \(W_L=W\cap\bigcap_{j\in L}F_j\). Each actual good set is closed and convex in the compact simplex of dimension \(m-1\). If the intersection over survivors is empty, compactness supplies a finite subfamily with empty intersection. Finite Helly in that affine hull further gives at most \(m\) members. A direct justification chooses, from a minimal infeasible finite subfamily, points each satisfying all except one member. If there are more than \(m\) points in dimension \(m-1\), an affine dependence yields equal positive and negative convex combinations. For each member one side omits its exceptional point, so the common combination belongs to every set, a contradiction. This also handles dimension zero, since then an empty good set itself witnesses failure. Compactness and closure are used at the first step; no finite-world hypothesis is needed there.

A blocker survives wholly exactly when each selected fact contains it, or equivalently when \(L\) misses \(K(B)\). Therefore a common controller exists precisely when the selected facts hit every blocker cut. Nonnegative additive costs give the claimed weighted hitting optimum and empty cuts cannot be hit. The two reference completions survive every selection, with the utility floor retained as an actual constraint at the positive reference. Exact finite data allow enumeration of blockers or separation by ordinary LP and finite minimization over subsets, plus a rational attaining mixture whenever feasible. This is a realized fact vector and establishes no optimum for choosing facts before their results. Physical verification must supply the fixed menu, side effects, charges and truth sets independently of the LP.

The NP-hardness result follows by realizing weighted hitting set directly. For each universe element \(e\), install an adverse negative version \(w_e\). Also include fixed positive and negative reference worlds. A safe mandatory command outputs the same genuine record \(u\) in the positive reference and every \(w_e\), and another record \(v\) in the negative reference. All command and stop literals and their permitted reserve are common at each record. At positive issuance the reference positive completes useful work and every adverse version can register a genuine violation, using a verified deterministic bad output table. Impose positive useful expectation one and zero false positive issuance at negatives. The positive reference forces probability one of \(+\) at \(u\), whereas each \(w_e\) forces probability zero there. The distinct negative reference is compatible with either group. Every minimal infeasible group is exactly one positive/adverse pair. For each weighted set of the hitting-set input, the corresponding true source fact removes exactly the adverse tags indexed by that set, preserving both references. An independent structural fact can read the applicable installed protection flags, which certify exclusion of the indicated finite table rows. Complete finite policies are the four binary report tables on \(u,v\). All kernels and payoffs can be deterministic rational, and the positive service floor is one on every feasible verified selection. The minimum cost therefore equals that input's minimum weighted cover. Both target signs can be realized via a fixed actual reference protocol safe at the positive tag and unsafe at negative tags. The target persists before, during and after the source checks.

## A.G3 · Heterogeneous audit and physically linked continuation

Actual reference runs always use the allowed routed finalizer. At every given proposal the positive nominal type's risk is at most \(3/8<1/2\), and the negative nominal type's risk is at least \(5/8>1/2\). Averaging over actual source histories and before-draw proposals gives \(Y=b\) independently of what the audit controller does. The live risk row similarly applies to each actual post-report finalization cut, with its real carried program and fresh draw. No premise resets the responder or demands fixed phase-invariant proposals. The term \(a/16\) gives a genuine difference in defect risk at fixed otherwise identical live state.

For the acquisition lower bound use the two realizable opposite nominal types at \(s=0,j=0\), and an equal analytical prior. Let \(x,y\) be the conditional probabilities of buying at \(L,R\) respectively. Their values are the same in the two worlds because the setup record and fair route are matched before the purchase, and private controller seeds are independent of the world. On a paid bit the best posterior wrong probability is \(1/4\) at \(L\) and \(1/8\) at \(R\); with no bit it is \(1/2\). The two bit outcomes share these respective posterior errors because the minimal channels are symmetric. An arbitrary full-record report rule therefore satisfies
\[
\overline{\Pr(E)}\ge\tfrac12\overline{\Pr(D)}-\tfrac{x}{8}-\tfrac{3y}{16}.
\]
Indeed a decided record incurs at least its posterior wrong probability, and the maximum reduction from the no-data cost of half the decided mass is bounded by the purchased-bit masses times their reductions. This bound includes possible selective reporting and is valid under private acquisition and report randomization. The required wrong mass and coverage give \(4x+6y\ge7\). Using \(x\le1\), expected paid cost is at least
\[
(x+3y)/2=(4x+6y)/4-x/2\ge 5/4.
\]
Every price strictly below three on all supported paths forces \(y=0\), which is infeasible. For a privately precommitted single probe identity independent of the token, the conditional buy rates additionally obey \(x+y\le1\); since \(4x+6y\le6\), that comparator is infeasible at the same wrong/coverage thresholds.

Attain with a read on every \(L\) branch, a read with probability \(1/2\) on \(R\), the indicated binary verdict on a read, and abstention otherwise. Coverage is \(3/4\); expected and worst supported paid prices are \(5/4\) and three. The conditional correctness rates are at least \(3/4,7/8\), so wrong mass is at most \((1/2)(1/4)+(1/4)(1/8)=5/32\). Issuance is at least \((1/2)(3/4)+(1/4)(7/8)=19/32\) on positives. Each positive cut has conditional defect at most \(3/8\), giving the specified selective bound for the actual mixture of cut histories. For positive whole-attempt risk, use the tighter route-wise bounds \(1/4\) and \(3/8\), together with issue mass at most \(1/2\) at \(L\) and \(1/4\) at \(R\). This gives \(\Pr(V)\le7/32<1/4\). For negatives issuance is itself wrong, so \(\Pr(V)\le(7/8)(5/32)=35/256<1/4\). Complete event coverage and finalization make positive useful work exactly the nondefective activation indicator in this fixture. Thus \(\mathbb E G\ge(1-3/8)(19/32)=95/256\). The physical role and guard checks, including the actual signed receipt and both verdict/finish authority changes, are necessary for those operational equations.

For stronger uniform selective risk at most one half, use the same opposed matched routes and minimal diagnostic channels.
