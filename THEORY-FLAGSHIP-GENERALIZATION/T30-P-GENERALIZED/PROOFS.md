## G01.1 · Exact linked semantics and robust certificates

### Sequence law and actual support

Fix the world. For each possible sequence let \(x_\sigma\) be the measure of private seeds prescribing its own actions on its specified records, whether or not chance reaches the records. At every record the seeds matching the predecessor partition by the next own choice. This gives the realization equalities. On a leaf whose own sequence the seed prescribes, all chance factors are the declared \(k_w(t)\), since the source responds to the invocations and their represented effects. Consequently the leaf probability, including on the marked calculation tree, is \(k_w(t)x_{\sigma(t)}\). Perfect recall ensures that records identified in this calculation have one predecessor sequence. This reasoning includes independent behavioral draws by including all needed private draws in the seed.

Conversely make the decision at \(h\) with positive predecessor weight by choosing \(a\) with probability \(x_{\sigma(h)a}/x_{\sigma(h)}\). Products of these ratios telescope along positive sequences. A zero sequence weight requires a first zero extension from a positive predecessor and hence no execution of that sequence. An arbitrary formal choice at a zero predecessor has no supported reach. These rules give the same leaf law in every fixed world. Each realization coordinate lies in \([0,1]\) by the flow equalities and nonnegativity along its predecessors. Thus \(X\) is compact. Ratios at a returned algebraic \(x\) are exact algebraic probabilities. Physical step counts here are the declared source moves. A concrete sampler with unlimited fair independent bits can compare its generated uniform binary expansion to the exact probability to successively certified precision, with almost surely finite comparison. A hard bound on sampler bits would change the policy class.

All marker contributions are nonnegative. For any possible forbidden invocation at a reached controller record, if its prescription has positive weight then that concrete checkpoint's positive chance prefix and the shared sequence factor yield a positive marked leaf. This includes an occurrence whose source-atomic freeze is broken by any positive-support successor: the entire occurrence receives the marker before chance. For a lawful call whose actual positive successor breaches a hard budget, the genuine row supplies that successor's factor before marking. Conversely, positive mass on a marker indicates a supported concrete hard failure. Zero mass exactly excludes every such path in this finite tree. Off-match hard obligations in later transport still depend on this full true support argument. Guarding all \(w\in W\) enforces one common policy on all admitted actual records and compatible checkpoints it reaches, with no assignment of physical effects to a forbidden call.

Finite products and sums of the value graphs in the statement are semialgebraic, since the result's graph is obtained by adding real variables for intermediate arithmetic gates and projecting. A rational graph with denominator \(g>0\) uses the equality \(g y=f\) in its domain; comparisons preserve their direction after multiplication by \(g\). Constants, terminal tags and possible zero-support or jump boundaries use their actual pieces. ### Exact decision and verification witnesses

For feasibility use the formula
\[
\exists x\left[x\in X\ \land\
 \forall w\left(w\in W\Rightarrow B_w(x)=0\right)\ \land\
 \forall w\left(w\in C\Rightarrow\bigwedge_\ell F_{\ell,w}(x)\le b_\ell\right)\right].
\]
Finite regime labels can be represented by disjunctions. An exact semialgebraic implementation uses elimination in real closed fields on the rational polynomial formula, including exact handling of strict conditions. An algebraic sample of the feasible set of \(x\) exists whenever it is nonempty, by transfer to the real closed field of real algebraic numbers, or equivalently by exact algebraic cell sampling. Opposite labels or a violating world at an algebraic \(x\) use the corresponding existential formula with an exact algebraic sample. If a class is a checked outer cover, these are witnesses of the represented problem; physical obstruction additionally requires their source realizability.

At a returned algebraic \(x\), the verifier can demand a finite emptiness proof for the violated-constraint set in each world scope. Write its Boolean formula in finite disjunctive sign-conjunction form. Convert \(g\ge0\) to \(g-z^2=0\), \(g>0\) to \(g z^2-1=0\), and \(g\ne0\) to \(g z-1=0\) using fresh real variables. Reverse signs for \(\le,<\) and keep equality constraints. This preserves feasibility for each case. The weak real Nullstellensatz over a real closed field gives an identity
\[
-1=\sum_i q_i^2+\sum_j u_j f_j
\]
for each empty equality case. Indeed such an identity is exactly the impossibility of a real common root, by the theorem in the other direction. Here the coefficient field can be the real algebraic numbers. Every identity is checkable by exact polynomial and algebraic-number arithmetic. A finite, possibly inefficient, construction after an emptiness decision enumerates finite exact algebraic polynomial lists by encoding length and identity-checks them until it finds a certificate. All searches halt on their proved empty cases. This supplies both a constructive algebraic decision and checkable exact proof objects. It singles out neither a uniform low-degree bound nor a particular fast quantifier-elimination implementation. [Real algebraic decision and Nullstellensatz references](LITERATURE.md)

For a fixed world, intersect its applicable constraints with \(X\) to obtain a closed convex set, using all guard constraints at \(w\in W\) and performance rows if \(w\in C\). If the total intersection is empty, compactness supplies an already empty finite subfamily. For nonempty \(X\), take a minimal such family of \(s\) worlds and choose \(x_j\in X\) satisfying all that family's constraints except those of world \(j\). If \(s>d_X+1\), there is a nontrivial affine dependence of the \(x_j\). Separate positive and negative coefficients, renormalize each set of coefficients to sum to one, and equate the two resulting convex combinations. For every world \(j\), at least one combination uses no \(x_j\), so the common point belongs to that world's convex feasible part. This contradicts emptiness. The witness size argument uses compactness of the policy domain, not of the set of worlds.

For recovery enumerate \(s=1,\ldots,d_X+1\) and ask for algebraic representatives \(w_1,\ldots,w_s\in W\) with no common \(x\in X\) under each world's appropriate constraints. This is one exact semialgebraic formula per \(s\); using transfer gives algebraic outputs at the first feasible size. A finite-dimensional linear infeasibility certificate over their real algebraic coefficients follows by the exact Farkas alternative, with equalities written as pairs when needed. A separate checker verifies every witness's original membership, all evaluated coefficients and the certificate. If \(X\) itself is empty, certify it directly. No new fictive worlds are needed.

For an algebraic controller the supremum of a bounded score over a nonempty class is specified by the conjunction of an upper bound for every member and a strict improvement over \(t-\delta\) for some member at every \(\delta>0\). This first-order formula picks out one algebraic value \(t\). Attainment has the further existential test \(F_w(x)=t\). Change the sign for the infimum. Compact parameter sets with continuous coefficients give actual extrema. Without those hypotheses the value and attainment tests remain distinct. The robust feasible policy set is closed in the compact \(X\). A bounded family of cost coefficient vectors makes its supremum Lipschitz in \(x\), because the difference of suprema is at most the supremum of differences, bounded by a uniform dual norm times \(\|x-y\|\). This gives attainment in policy optimization whether or not the inner extremum is attained. The first-order optimization graph, or the minimum robust epigraph cap with bounded range, gives exact algebraic value and optimal controller. Worst utility is treated by negation.

## G01.2 · Approximation, genuine enlargement and complexity

The exact soft constraint implies its sampled counterparts. Conversely, any feasible sample point's controller obeys the original soft constraint to at most \(Lr\) excess by the verified comparison. Tightening every sample cap by \(Lr\) therefore ensures all true weak caps. All three tests use the same exact hard guard. For a sample-feasible policy \(x\) and a uniformly strictly feasible hard-legal \(x_0\), set \(\lambda=Lr/(s+Lr)\). The mixture \((1-\lambda)x+\lambda x_0\) stays in the convex hard guard and its maximum excess is at most \((1-\lambda)Lr-\lambda s=0\). Linear cost increases by at most \(\lambda D\). This compares precisely the same realized policy decisions over worlds. A safe bound \(Lr\) can be independently proved directly, for example with rational gradient bounds on a convex parameter domain where every intervening denominator is away from zero, or checked as a quantified semialgebraic inequality. Hard guard membership is never settled by a numerical mesh.

The following instances prove the stated strict changes and boundaries.

- **Nonpolyhedral linked laws.** A fixed \(\theta\in[0,1]\) governs two successive fresh conditional Bernoulli draws. Write \(m=\Pr(X_1=1)=\theta\) and \(v=\Pr(X_1=X_2=1)=\theta^2\). For every \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), the linear form \(v-2\alpha m\) has its unique minimizing pair at \((\alpha,\alpha^2)\). These remain distinct exposed points of the convex hull. The convex hull is compact and has infinitely many exposed points. The projected hull of a finite fixed-component mixture polytope is itself a polytope, so it cannot give this set or its complete family of affine support values. The true event probability of differing draws is \(2\theta(1-\theta)=1/2-2(\theta-1/2)^2\). Endpoints both score zero; a spurious independent row selection of \(\theta_1=0,\theta_2=1\) gives one. The draws can each reveal a lawful different receipt without changing these probabilities. Work can complete on all branches independently of the event.
- **Irrational common policy with rational input.** Let \(0\le\theta\le1\), \(2\theta^2\ge1\), and \(\alpha=1/\sqrt2\). Force one selection from two lawful commands, let \(x\) be probability of the first. The expected violation and useful service scores are \(1/2+(x-\theta)/2\) and \(1/2+(x-1/(2\theta))/2\). These are realized by giving each command two independently scored physical Bernoulli outcomes with those probabilities at \(x=0,1\), for example an independent hazard and a separate work completion. All probabilities are in \([0,1]\), and \(\theta>0\) in the admitted compact domain. A risk cap \(1/2\) demands \(x\le\min\theta=\alpha\). A utility floor \(1/2\) demands \(x\ge\max_\theta 1/(2\theta)=\alpha\). The unique feasible controller has algebraic irrational mixing probability.
- **No finite world collection preserves the full optimum.** Let \(x\in[0,1]\), \(\theta\in[0,1]\), and \(V\) have probability \(1/2+(\theta x-\theta^2)/2\), obtained by mixing two actual normalized one-command rows. The at-most-\(1/2\) constraint requires \(\theta x\le\theta^2\) for all \(\theta\), hence \(x=0\). Every finite selection with a positive \(\theta\) permits a strictly positive \(x\) up to the smallest selected positive \(\theta\). If only zero is selected all \(x\) are feasible. Thus no finite sample preserves the exact feasible set or the exact optimum of maximizing \(x\), despite compactness and continuity. On the noncompact domain \(x\in(0,1]\), every finite subsystem still permits a policy but the full constraint is infeasible.
- **Unattained extrema on compact parameters.** Let the actual fixed-command event probability be \(\theta\) for \(0\le\theta<1\), and zero at \(\theta=1\), a rational semialgebraic two-piece row. Its supremum is one and no actual member realizes it. A strict sign approached at a discontinuity is distinct from an attained observed value.
- **Hardness.** From an unweighted Simple Max Cut instance with \(m\) edges and integer threshold \(k\), choose an edge uniformly after the single protected command. Conditional on that edge \((i,j)\), make two draws with success probabilities \(\theta_i,\theta_j\), both governed throughout by the fixed vector in \([0,1]^n\). Their disagreement is the violation and the work reward is always one. The expanded chance tree has \(O(m)\) bounded-depth branches and degree-two outcome probabilities. Its risk is \(\sum_{(i,j)}(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\theta_i\theta_j)/m\). This function is affine separately in each coordinate. Push one coordinate at a time to an endpoint without decreasing it, so a maximum is a box vertex and equals maximum cut size divided by \(m\). Risk at most \((k-1)/m\) for all parameters is the complement of the Simple Max Cut decision. This is coNP-hard with a singleton work policy and constant move/chance depth. No general polynomial-time approximation with unqualified additive error, or stronger complexity classification, follows from this reduction alone. [Simple Max Cut primary source](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1)

## G02.1 · Local evidence yields two-sided actual bounds

This uses each genuine conditional row for the full relevant local state, and complete match timing and records. Marginal agreement after forgetting previous receipts or carry would not suffice. Integrate over the independent seed to get the declared \(\mu_{w,x}\). Its leaves have weights equal to the products of pair subrow factors times the shared sequence weight, and the two marginal laws are dominated pointwise.

Each residual marginal measure has mass \(1-m\) and expected score contribution between \(L(1-m)\) and \(U(1-m)\). Subtracting nominal residual from actual residual gives the first interval around the signed matched difference \(s\). Alternatively, apply the independent actual payoff interval at each matched terminal and bound only the actual residual. This gives the second interval. Use survival factors \(q_j=1-\gamma_j\) in \([0,1]\), including the initial factor. If a supplied lower bound is negative, replace it by zero because genuine subrow mass is nonnegative. Induction then gives the product lower bound. Multiplying raw negative lower bounds would be invalid. An unchecked branch can also be modeled by a true complete row and a zero corresponding subrow without changing actual execution or authority.

Use normalized thresholds \(\varepsilon,u\in[0,1]\), so the ranges below include the zero score upon nonactivation. ## G02.3 · Dependent calibration, stopping and physical risk

Fix a true admitted world. Regard the global audit as discrete time and let \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) contain the lawful record and any current private randomness determining the next selection before its diagnostic result. For each registered diagnostic \(j\) the selection indicator \(A_{j,t}\) is predictable. Let \(\mu_t=\mathbb E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\) for a taken diagnostic and choose a dummy zero increment otherwise. Put \(N_{j,t}=\sum_{k\le t}A_{j,k}\) and \(S_{j,t}=\sum_{k\le t}A_{j,k}(X_k-\mu_k)\). The conditional bounded-draw estimate is
\[
\mathbb E[\exp(\lambda(X_t-\mu_t))\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le e^{\lambda^2/8}.
\]
For completeness, condition on the past. Convexity of the exponential bounds the moment of \(X_t\in[0,1]\) by that of a Bernoulli variable of the same mean. The log centered moment of that Bernoulli variable is zero with zero slope at the origin and has second derivative at most \(1/4\), since a Bernoulli variance under exponential tilting is at most \(1/4\). Integration gives the estimate for both signs of \(\lambda\).

Consequently \(M_{j,t}=\exp(\lambda S_{j,t}-\lambda^2 N_{j,t}/8)\) is a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. For any \(c>0\), stopping at its first crossing of \(c\) or a finite deterministic time bounds the crossing probability by \(1/c\). Letting the finite time grow proves the same bound over all times. If the \(n\)-th selected result exists and \(S_j\) there is at least \(nr\), using \(\lambda=4r\) gives crossing probability at most \(e^{-2nr^2}\); use the negative sign for the lower tail. This treats a possibly never reached \(n\)-th sample and does not condition on sample count in a way that changes coverage. The average of the actual conditional means of the first \(n\) such draws is within \(b_j\) of \(m_j(w)\). Spending \(2e^{-2nr(n)^2}=\eta/[k n(n+1)]\) and summing over all registered streams and counts gives one simultaneous exception event of probability at most \(\eta\). Outward enclosure and clipping preserve inclusion. No independent streams or identical means are needed. Exact encoded scores and interval bounds preserve algebraic decision; real-valued observations without an exact encoding only give the mathematical probability guarantee unless an additional valid rounding rule is supplied.

At the lawful quota point on coverage, every retained world and actual world have each mean inside the same \(j\)-interval of width at most \(2(b_j+\bar r(n_j))\). They are therefore in the neighborhood from the premise. Its correct verdict and possible uniform protected controller still work on the smaller class. An earlier permitted verdict is likewise valid on coverage. The guarantee uses exhaustion of all required lawful quotas apart from an earlier permitted issuance. Without such a margin, no uniform successful stop follows. In particular, two opposite worlds with an identical entire permitted audit law remain indistinguishable, and bias bands with overlap can realize such a law by a common fixed conditional mean when the admitted instrument permits it.

For composition take the union of calibration failure and any separately warranted target-error event, which has probability at most \(\eta+\delta\). Exact labels on covered sets charge no additional \(\delta\). At each fixed actual world and its good stopped record, use the checked fresh actual law conditional on that record and selected policy for the physical selective and utility inequalities. Bad records can contribute at most \(\Pr(I\mid\text{record})\) to physical violation and no guaranteed utility. The expected activated bad-record mass is at most \(\min(a,\eta+\delta)\). Summing the good and bad contributions gives
\[
\Pr(V\cap I)\le\varepsilon(a-e)+e,\qquad
\mathbb E[IG]\ge u(a-e)
\]
with \(e=\min(a,\eta+\delta)\). This inequality is unconditional on issuance. Selection can concentrate errors among activations. Independent exact hard authority remains checked on the full guard even on statistical failure, and any physical calibration hazards within the target would need to be carried and scored in those rows rather than treated as safe.

## G02.4 · Nonzero selective risk with unverified residual law

Use the common start action. On a positive installation the actual mass of harmless completed activations is \(4/5\), and that of completed violating activation \(1/20\). A known \(1/20\) nonactivation is common. The two signed exact checks supply independently addressed routine tags, each implemented by a disjoint set of tickets of lengths 12 and 4 in a 20-ticket draw. The known bad and inactive tags use one ticket each; the last two tickets enter the unchecked branch. With both facts the common mass is \(9/10\), common activation \(17/20\), common joint violating activation \(1/20\), and common useful activation \(17/20\). G02.1 for \(\varepsilon=3/19\) has upper signed risk
\[
1/20-(3/19)(17/20)+(1/10)(1-3/19)=0.
\]
For \(u=17/19\) the lower signed utility is
\[
(17/20)(1-17/19)-(1/10)(17/19)=0.
\]

The unknown conditional draws remain unchecked and varying.
