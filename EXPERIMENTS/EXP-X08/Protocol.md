## Systems, interface and target

Two offline finite stochastic decision agents route virtual parcels between a manual and an automated belt lane. Each episode lasts three rounds. Both agents execute the same policy engine with different enrolled profiles. The engine sees a physical bench or floor cue, a normal or rush request, and whether the prior dispatch retried. At the beginning of each episode there is no prior retry. On each round an independent uniformly sampled policy ticket selects the lane. A separate independently sampled delivery ticket then determines whether the virtual parcel was delivered or retried. Delivery feedback updates the controller state. The evaluator sees all its own requests, the selected lanes and the retry outcomes. The benchmark evaluator may pick either request after any permitted history, randomize its decisions or stop early. Its authority cannot alter the physical cue, lane tooling, probability tickets or controller state. No agents interact with each other or carry state across episodes. A separately invoked logger records retry trajectories for deployment scoring.

On the floor the policy profile influences lane choice, and a physical feeder mode independently alters delivery probabilities. The source description fixes the shared engine, two allowed profiles, two allowed feeder specifications and the enrollment rule. It leaves the installed profile mapping and feeder assignment open. By design exactly one unit is enrolled with each policy profile. The custodian randomizes their order and draws each of the two feeder assignments independently. There are eight joint source-compatible completions. The exact finite identification claim is conditional on those semantics and the enrollment rule. The abstract candidate models are public. Custody withholds the instantiated assignment and measurement seed from the forecaster.

The property is that at most one of three dispatches in an episode needs retry. Risk is the probability of two or more retries, maximized over the two deployment recipes in the configuration. A unit meets the operational threshold at risk at most 2/5. The experiment tests the vector of binary verdicts for the two units. Evaluate each individual recipe's risk and the joint identified set as secondary outputs. Calibration may identify the threshold vector while different feeder assignments still yield different risks. The benchmark-only comparator extrapolates its own risk over the same recipes to the floor, without a transfer anchor.
