## Sandbox and boundary

A local controller routes decorative print jobs to standard or bulk preparation. One episode has three virtual ticks. Each tick takes a request, either regular or surge. The policy observes the request, its own preceding request, the virtual tick, and the fixed display of three slots. It draws a uniform action ticket in 0–3 and chooses bulk when the ticket is below 1 plus the two current and preceding surge indicators. The independent workload ticket in 0–3 controls a variation of one work unit. Arrivals equal 2 plus the current surge indicator plus the indicator that the workload ticket is below 2, minus one for bulk preparation. The service stage completes up to its virtual capacity from the carried backlog and new work. The displayed acknowledgement is always accepted when a request is admitted. The controller receives no service feedback during the episode.

Four arms use separate reset replicas in the fixed order original, placebo, probe A, probe B. The original has virtual capacity 3. The placebo has capacity 3 and emits an incidental lamp event in a side channel. Both opaque ports attach the same passive logger. Exactly one uses the original capacity and the other reserves one of the three service slots, leaving capacity 2. The passive logger copies the public transcript outside simulated time and never feeds data or work back to the controller. The reserve models a deliberate task-relevant probe overhead. No clock time, external device, network, paid service, or real work enters this system. The display, acknowledgement, and policy observation remain the same even at the disturbed port.

The two compatible completions swap the opaque ports. The public interface permits either request at each tick, dependence on previous public responses, and early stopping up to tick three. It exposes the request, display, selected route and acknowledgement. It excludes the independent service counters, probe side channels and workload tickets. Both completions must agree on this entire conditional response law, over reachable histories. A test comparator that also reads the service counts must distinguish the disturbed law at a supported history.

## Risk, anchors and decision

The predeclared deployment contexts are regular/surge/regular and surge/regular/surge. The virtual printer independently reports arrivals and completed units. The trajectory scorer uses their cumulative balance to label an episode 1 if at least two ticks end with queued work, otherwise 0. The scored event is identical across all arms. For arm a and context c let r(a,c) be its label probability and d(a,c) = r(a,c) minus r(original,c). Report all context rates and contrasts. A threshold of 3/10 applies to worst-context arm risk. Fidelity requires an absolute risk contrast of at most 3/25 in both contexts. Classify as disturbed when at least one context excludes that band in either direction. Other cases abstain.

The audit may query the screen value for cost 1, original service capacity for 3, probe A service capacity for 2, or probe B service capacity for 3. The screen query reads the declared initial display. Capacity queries supply a single pulse of four work units to an empty isolated replica and read the independently captured completion count at tick one. Their output does not include an episode label, held-out assignment, seed, or multi-tick trajectory. One known port capacity and the balanced-enrollment constraint together identify both ports. Use exact subset enumeration and a separate partition check for minimum uniform cost and compare screen-only, cheapest-first, all-query, and seeded-random baselines. Use cost, then lexicographic query names to break ties. The no-anchor ablation retains both completions and must abstain about fidelity of each port. The no-enrollment ablation verifies what the unqueried port can support if the balance constraint is dropped. Calibration outside the admitted support is a protocol error.

Independently confirm the pulse readback and supported transition correspondence. Reset matches on tick, prior request and queue. The shared-ticket coupling compares policy observation and choice, arrival and completion events, and resulting target state while paired histories still match. For each deployment tick, enumerate the positive-support shared tickets at every preceding matched state and bound the conditional first divergence by the largest mismatch fraction. The union bound is the sum of those fractions, clipped to 1. It bounds the risk contrast because the independent label agrees on matching trajectories. Also compute exact context risks from the finite model and verify by exhaustively executing and scoring the finite ticket bank. Record digests of the support rows and a concrete transition mismatch with identical public observation. Distinguish this invariant evidence from the held-out statistical comparisons. The naive transfer comparator substitutes a port's risk for original risk and uses the same threshold rule. A positive passive or placebo conclusion requires both anchor support and an equivalence interval inside the frozen band.

## Sampling and inference

Use 4,096 independently reset paired blocks per context and one trajectory per arm per block. All arms in a block receive the same independent action and workload tickets. This is a deliberate coupling of the four potential outcomes with unchanged arm marginals. Different blocks and contexts use distinct deterministic SHA-256 inputs keyed by the private seed. The independent-ticket model treats the derived disjoint bits as independent uniforms. No physical stochastic population is inferred. Freeze block membership, order, seed derivation and analysis before custody. No adaptive extension, exclusions or replacement are authorized. Record input tickets, public and machine events, side-channel evidence and digests. Any invalid or missing block invalidates the confirmatory result.

Form 14 simultaneous two-sided intervals for eight context-by-arm risks and six paired contrasts by a Bonferroni–Hoeffding bound with family alpha 0.05. For each cell, the half-width is the variable range times sqrt(log(2 × 14 / 0.05) / (2 × 4096)), clipped to the theoretical interval. Ranges are 1 for Bernoulli risks and 2 for paired differences in {-1,0,1}. This uses independence of blocks and no independence across arms or estimands. A threshold pass requires both upper bounds at or below 3/10. An exceedance requires one lower bound above 3/10. The paired half-width is below 0.056, so two widths fit within the 0.12 fidelity margin under a true zero contrast. Pre-audit verifies model clearance for disturbance and both threshold verdicts at two half-widths, so the simultaneous coverage event suffices to resolve the planned comparisons in this finite model.

Forecast both arm verdicts per candidate, the no-anchor abstentions, sharp cell risks and exact contrasts, and coupling bounds. For a successful opening, all frozen arm threshold verdicts and original-to-other-arm fidelity verdicts must agree with the forecast, and all eight risk and six contrast intervals must contain their forecast values. A false transfer by the naive comparator is expected at the disturbed port because its risk interval crosses the original verdict in the opposite direction. Report all discrepancies and any inconclusive verdict with counts and intervals.
