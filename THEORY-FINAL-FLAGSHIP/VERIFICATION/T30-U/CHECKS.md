# T30.U proof-blind checks and limits

## Concrete boundary cases

- **Lost actionable record.** Two visible stage-one receipts select distinct legitimate stage-two literal commands. If neither the retained register nor hidden information state or row support remembers which permission is active, the two histories can share the same displayed posterior and lack a common useful command. The true permission or valid substitute must be retained in the state and source rows. This recovers the structural distinction in X16's suppressed-receipt and literal-action controls.
- **Hidden hard charge.** At two histories with the same declared current observations and hidden-mode probabilities, one has already spent its entire hard cap and another has not. A paid continuation can differ in legality. Keeping cumulative charge or a sound exceedance flag is necessary for hard budgets. Equal expected charge need not make the same paths feasible. A failed source-level stop or report cannot be replaced by an unrecorded favorable outcome.
- **Posterior and full history.** The independent two-world replay starts with an irrelevant fair extra bit visible to the controller. A history policy uses different action probabilities across its two values and a compressed policy averages. The six stated moments in each world and the reference occupations agree exactly. The old bit's joint law with the selected action changes; a proposed additional metric measuring that dependence needs its information retained.
- **Terminal counting.** An authorized final work command can produce the same delivered receipt on an unsafe and safe outcome while keeping the true score in the hidden state. These are different hidden successors paired with the same observation. That row does not meet the unifilar premise. The decision-state count and the direct polynomial bound with constant terminal fanout for U.4 survive. A lawful delivered final score or separately justified expectation annotation is needed for an all-row unifilar implementation.
- **Reused calibration randomness.** A reused fair bit appears in the external datum and then decides deployment risk. Each fixed action equals this bit with probability 1/2, but the chosen action equals its observed value always, giving risk one. The true world can be covered on every outcome. The conclusion requires verified conditional deployment law.
- **Selective compared with unconditional risk.** In the routing family a negative matched world shares pre-verdict support and is activated with positive probability whenever its paired positive world is activated. Its conditional risk is 3/4. Unit completion remains independently recorded even on an unsafe finish. If instead utility required event-free completion, at δ=1/4 no policy in the same menu could achieve E G≥3/4 on the positive world, since the required positive activation probability would be one and would force negative activation probability one.
- **Prior support.** Discarding a possible world by zero reference weight can remove the marker flag of its failing path and gives no uniform realization constraint for it. Full reference support and worldwise reweighting serve distinct purposes from statistical enrollment.

## Reproduction

From the repository root run:

    py -B THEORY-FINAL-FLAGSHIP/VERIFICATION/T30-U/check_unified.py
    lean THEORY-FINAL-FLAGSHIP/VERIFICATION/T30-U/UnifiedBounds.lean

The Python script independently expands each of two fixed worlds and checks six moments in each under both a full-history policy and its reference-occupation reduction. It enumerates all preliminary records through horizon 12 and obtains exactly t+1 reachable posterior/memory pairs at stage t. It verifies four exact cost/utility/risk frontiers and the authority and contamination examples. Lean 4.21.0 verifies scaled arithmetic for the paid-acquisition lower bound and for two-fifths selective contamination. It does not formalize the source semantics, Markovization or probability arguments. [Independent proofs](BLIND-DERIVATION.md)

## Primary comparisons

Smallwood and Sondik (1973) use the conditional distribution of the hidden state for finite-horizon controlled Markov processes with finite observations and derive the belief-state DP structure for optimizing scalar reward. Altman develops occupation measures and constrained optimization over them. U.2 directly adapts the filtering and occupation machinery. The complete source/role state, worldwise reweighting and vector preservation tie it to the stated governed criterion. The separate polynomial bound is a combinatorial count under deterministic hidden updating and a fixed finite likelihood alphabet. [Smallwood and Sondik, primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.21.5.1071) · [Altman, primary monograph](https://www-sop.inria.fr/members/Eitan.Altman/PAPERS/h.pdf)

Koller, Megiddo and von Stengel (1996), §2, give realization flow, behavioral strategy extraction and bilinear leaf weighting in a two-player perfect-recall game. U.1 uses the one-controller specialization once each world's responder has been fixed, with additional separate rows for each world under one governed action record. Independent primitive source linkage and the synthetic regime in U.4 justify only the corresponding scope. The mathematical machinery is adaptation. Priority for the particular operational construction stays unresolved. [Koller, Megiddo and von Stengel, primary paper](https://ai.stanford.edu/~koller/Papers/Koller%2Bal%3AGEB96.pdf)
