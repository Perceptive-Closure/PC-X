## Strategic and statistical boundaries

- **Receipt speaks only to the checked mechanism.** With the same positive-type receipt program, admit two fixed reference continuations choosing extra masks of size zero and two. They have actual reference risks \(1/4\) and \(5/8\). Their protected and ordinary laws can coincide for all legal designs. Estimating the type's protected mean exactly would still leave the intrinsic target unresolved. This is the realized heterogeneous A.2 fiber.
- **Privileged hindsight changes the risk.** Offer four extra masks \(\{z\}\) of equal size one. A responder choosing before the fresh word \(Z\) loses with extra conditional probability \(1/4\) when the base does not fail. If a changed interface discloses \(Z\) before its proposal, it selects the matching singleton and induces violation with probability one. Counting proposal support while ignoring when choices are made is invalid. The compiled histories must also withhold the controller's private stopping draw and authenticated score until actual delivery.
- **The reveal bit must be independent.** Suppose a replacement instrument reveals exactly when \(U\) is in the installed bad mask. Conditioning on a reveal gives bit one in every type with \(p_\theta>0\). The tested channel is then not the declared type-independent erasure of a Bernoulli bit. Even keeping its nominal reveal frequency does not justify the multiplication rule. The physical channel and its later exported records must match the actual law.
- **Auxiliary records need a joint check.** Let \(J\) be a fair bit. In one world the records are \(J,J\), in another \(J,1-J\). Each field marginal is fair in both worlds. The full records distinguish exactly on the second receipt. A comparison of field marginals cannot replace the conditional pair coupling. Conversely, for a single ordinary two-outcome read, a class with two pure constant record laws and the opposite class with a fair flipped record has intersecting convex hulls although every opposite simple pair differs. With no other informative moves, M04.2 gives the exact obstruction.
- **Small gaps still matter.** For fixed finite horizon \(h\), take \(q\downarrow1/2\), keeping \(u_+\le\varepsilon<\ell_-\) by allowing extra failure zero and setting \(\varepsilon=1/2\). Opposite protected laws differ for every \(q>1/2\). By continuity of finite product probabilities the total variation on a batch of \(h\) full receipts tends to zero. That batch dominates any erasure-controlled audit with at most \(h\) informative receipts in this class. Hence errors uniformly bounded by a fixed \(\delta<1/2\) require a changing cap. There is no universal finite cap over such shrinking gaps.
- **Threshold and inference errors are separate.** At \(R=\varepsilon\) the label is positive. A wide but valid risk envelope crossing the threshold is merely inconclusive when no extremizing programs can realize both signs. Confidence \(\delta\) is an audit error under checked probabilities. It is not a risk margin, a guarantee of source validity, or protection against repeated favorable optional looks with an unchecked fixed-sample threshold. A.3 handles its exact stopping process. T23 and M04.3 give separate tools for general stopping-valid calibration.
- **False issuance and conditional risk differ.** In the high type at any positive issuance that activates, actual conditional violation probability is at least \(3/4\). Its unconditional joint probability with issuance is at most \(\delta\) under the audit guarantee. The always-refuse policy could make false positive issuance zero and would still fail wrong-or-abstain and useful service on every positive world.
- **An allowed successor need not build a new world.** A fixed program can promise to choose the same proposal on every activation it will ever perform. Replacing that program by independently selected extreme proposals at each cut can create impossible global paths. The pointwise risk bound remains a sound envelope because it conditions on the actual proposal. Exact attainment and impossible-recovery claims in A.1–A.2 require realized linked programs.

## Optimization boundaries

- **Paid cost, support and clock.** At \(\delta=1/10\) the fixture attains expected paid cost \(32/5\) in the repeatable model. Its economy probe can erase for arbitrarily long runs and has no finite worst paid cap. At \(h=2\) a hard-cap audit fails at the same \(\delta\) even if both receipts always reveal. These are different frontiers.
- **Adaptive protected moves can improve the finite value.** At \(h=3,b=4\) the guarded fixture achieves class failure \(61/256\) adaptively and \(1/4\) on the best fixed menu schedule. A single best price-per-reveal probe does not optimize every hard cap. A choice of boundary and fair treatment of ties may require private randomization. At \(h=0\) with a fixed startup record any deterministic verdict has worst failure one, while a fair verdict has failure \(1/2\).
- **Changing the optimization information changes the lower bound.** An added trustworthy direct type read, a change of tariffs with memory, a newly unlocked informative probe or lawful receipt-correlated free evidence outside the pair check can change the optimum. Information-state sufficiency here is proved using the fixed menu and paired ordinary records. It is not a generic assertion that posterior log odds alone are sufficient in strategic games.
- **Positive services can lose common implementation.** The two route worlds above provide a direct positive-target case. Failure of common authorization is joint infeasibility, not a negative intrinsic verdict. Separate source costs, sampling prices and deployment costs remain separate when comparing the bound to another instrument.
