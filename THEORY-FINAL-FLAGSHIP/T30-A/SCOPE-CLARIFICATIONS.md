# T30.A scope clarifications after blind review

The original [statement seal](https://github.com/Perceptive-Closure/PC-X/blob/6b8469acbc837a98f13f0d7f2a217db47d46effd/THEORY-FINAL-FLAGSHIP/T30-A/STATEMENTS.md) is retained exactly. Apply A.1–A.4 with the following explicit conditions, which the finite fixture satisfies. [Independent derivation](https://github.com/Perceptive-Closure/PC-X/blob/eba38eedf9cac25c9545251c2e20d23103e18b20/THEORY-FINAL-FLAGSHIP/VERIFICATION/T30-A/BLIND-DERIVATION.md)

1. **Verdict authority.** From every optional decision record at which a rule can stop, both binary verdict commands are lawful physical occurrences. A positive verdict includes the reserved, lawful, immediate activation protocol. A negative verdict can lawfully finish, even though it is counted as an error on a positive world. The lower bound tolerates additional stop constraints but an attaining recurrence may then be illegal. An authorized abstention, if available, incurs failure in both classes. This condition also permits a fair private tie or zero-probe guess.
2. **Event coverage.** The claimed whole-attempt probability uses \(V_D\) as the exact aggregate violation within an activated attempt, or verifies that every earlier and other deployment step, including verdict and activation, is separately safe. Audit, preamble and nonactivation paths are likewise safe. If an unscored physical event elsewhere in the protocol can violate the declared property, the bound applies to \(V_D\) alone until that event is accounted for.
3. **Hard and expected reserves.** When the designer can choose a finite cap for A.2, the source must admit the resulting whole schedule under all other fixed constraints. A.3's expected-cost frontier uses its own checked repeatable model with no finite bound barring that rule. Even an exact expected cost cannot serve as a supported-path resource limit.

The first two conditions narrow the operational reading for acceptance without changing the numerical proofs. [Counterexamples](COUNTEREXAMPLES.md)
